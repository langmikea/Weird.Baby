# THE HOLES — what the story has not decided

**A fact that does not exist is as useful as one that does, and this page is the
list of them.** Every row here is **ABSENT — not zero, not none, not "no."** The
distinction is load-bearing: *the cell has no stated capacity* is a hole; *the
cell has no capacity* would be a fact, and a false one.

**Nothing here is filled in.** Where the corpus names a thing and never says
what it is, the row says so and stops.

Three kinds:

- **DEMANDED** — a document, a table position or a built surface asks for this
  and nothing answers. It is a gap somebody will hit.
- **UNASKED** — nothing needs it today. It is written down so it is not
  discovered as a surprise later.
- **MEASUREMENT** — not an authoring gap. Somebody has to go and measure the
  object.

Holes are numbered `H-nn` here. Upstream ids are printed where they exist.

## H-03 · THE FAR END
<a id="h-03"></a>

**DEMANDED.** The manual's SP 7-14 is **written and published** (`scan-11-a` /
`scan-11-b`, Record 003). It establishes that a far end exists, that it is a
station in its own right drawn on its own glass, that the link is bi-directional
by design, and — the instruction the whole page turns on —

> **"Assume the far end is attended."**

**WHAT IS ABSENT.**

- **How many far ends there are.** No figure anywhere. Not one, not several, not
  a network.
- **Where a far end is.** No location, no address, no installation.
- **Who or what attends one.** The manual says *assume attended*, which is an
  instruction to the operator and not a statement about the world.
- **What passes over the link.** The manual says the instrument's record *"holds
  that a link was opened and for how long. It does not hold what passed over
  it."*
- **What the installation record is, physically, or where it lives.** Named in
  B-1 as where the four communications values are entered. Nothing describes the
  object.
- **What happens at the far end when the near end is wrong.** See
  [FAILURE-MODES](FAILURE-MODES.md).

**AND THE STORY'S OWN LAW MAKES ONE OF THESE PERMANENT.** `THE_STORY_ARC.md`
layer 3: *"WHO OR WHAT is on the other end is DELIBERATELY UNANSWERED. Canon
never confirms autonomy, never denies presence."* Amendment 2 sharpens it:
*"NOBODY is at headquarters."* **So the identity of the far end is a sealed
hole, not an open one.** The count and the location are not covered by that seal
and are simply absent.

**AND RECORD 004 — WRITTEN, POSTING AT 17:00 ON 2026-08-20 — DESCRIBES A
BENCH.** *"Console is a single
welded steel desk. No maker plate, no model number… Nothing here postdates 1969.
Everything here works."* That is the **far end's furniture**, published, in
detail — and it still does not say where it is, how many there are, or whether
anybody sits at it. The Record's own summary of it is *"It appears to be an
unattended remote access terminal"* — which is the museum's reading, and the
manual says the opposite. Both are published. Neither is a ruling.

## H-07 · SHUTTING DOWN
<a id="h-07"></a>

**DEMANDED.** *Upstream: holes report `D-9`.*

Manual **¶5-17** exists as a position titled SHUTTING DOWN. **No owner-facing
power-off or sleep procedure exists anywhere in the corpus.**

**WHAT EXISTS AND IS NOT IT:**

- ¶7-19, written: *"When the instrument is off, the power control alone remains
  live. No input is read, no sound is made, and no part of the display system is
  driven. An instrument that answers any other control while off is not off, and
  is to be withdrawn from service."* — **that is a test for whether it is off,
  not a procedure for turning it off.**
- The CRT lifecycle law (Mike, 2026-07-24): every screen-off is a CRT death —
  collapse to a horizontal line, then a point, then out. That is the
  *presentation* of an off, fleet-wide.
- `STORY_BIBLE.html`: *"Sleep-return (short) is the dominant wake pattern; reboot
  happens sometimes."* — a statement about frequency, not a procedure.
- The 65's SLEEP / screensaver is named in the porch capture and is not specced.

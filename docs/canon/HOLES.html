THE HOLES — what the story has not decided

A fact that does not exist is as useful as one that does, and this page is the list of them. Every row here is ABSENT — not zero, not none, not "no." The distinction is load-bearing: the cell has no stated capacity is a hole; the cell has no capacity would be a fact, and a false one.

Nothing here is filled in. Where the corpus names a thing and never says what it is, the row says so and stops.

Three kinds:

  • DEMANDED — a document, a table position or a built surface asks for this and nothing answers. It is a gap somebody will hit.
  • UNASKED — nothing needs it today. It is written down so it is not discovered as a surprise later.
  • MEASUREMENT — not an authoring gap. Somebody has to go and measure the object.

Holes are numbered H-nn here. Upstream ids are printed where they exist.

H-01 · THE FOUR PERSONNEL FOLDERS

WHAT IS ABSENT. For all four: no name of a person, no dates, no photograph on file, no document text, no explanation of why personnel folders travel with a machine's manual, and no statement of what a personnel folder IS in this world.

H-03 · THE FAR END

DEMANDED. The manual's SP 7-14 is written and published (scan-11-a / scan-11-b, Record 003). It establishes that a far end exists, that it is a station in its own right drawn on its own glass, that the link is bi-directional by design, and — the instruction the whole page turns on —

"Assume the far end is attended."

WHAT IS ABSENT.

  • How many far ends there are. No figure anywhere. Not one, not several, not a network.
  • Where a far end is. No location, no address, no installation.
  • Who or what attends one. The manual says assume attended, which is an instruction to the operator and not a statement about the world.
  • What passes over the link. The manual says the instrument's record "holds that a link was opened and for how long. It does not hold what passed over it."
  • What the installation record is, physically, or where it lives. Named in B-1 as where the four communications values are entered. Nothing describes the object.
  • What happens at the far end when the near end is wrong. See FAILURE-MODES.

AND THE STORY'S OWN LAW MAKES ONE OF THESE PERMANENT. THE_STORY_ARC.md layer 3: "WHO OR WHAT is on the other end is DELIBERATELY UNANSWERED. Canon never confirms autonomy, never denies presence." Amendment 2 sharpens it: "NOBODY is at headquarters." So the identity of the far end is a sealed hole, not an open one. The count and the location are not covered by that seal and are simply absent.

AND RECORD 004 — WRITTEN, POSTING AT 17:00 ON 2026-08-20 — DESCRIBES A BENCH. "Console is a single welded steel desk. No maker plate, no model number… Nothing here postdates 1969. Everything here works." That is the far end's furniture, published, in detail — and it still does not say where it is, how many there are, or whether anybody sits at it. The Record's own summary of it is "It appears to be an unattended remote access terminal" — which is the museum's reading, and the manual says the opposite. Both are published. Neither is a ruling.

H-04 · THE TELEVISION

DEMANDED. Upstream: robots vocabulary §8.

The corpus has a bezel, an opening, a feed, eight engraved channels and a source dial. It has no receiver.

WHAT IS ABSENT: nothing names the glass the feed lands on, its size, its type, its warm-up, its own controls, or its catalogue number.

WHAT EXISTS AND IS NOT IT:

  • The bezel — canon 2026-07-29: renders as its own layer above everything and takes no video treatment whatsoever, because it is the real world's frame.
  • The FEED CONTROL — a badge bolted to a machine: ABEAL · FEED CONTROL · MODEL NO. TYPE 8p. SER. NO. and DATE are struck wells with nothing in them, deliberately.
  • The drum — eight engraved channels, of which two arm.

The FEED CONTROL selects a feed. It is not the thing the feed appears on.

H-05 · TWO ACRONYMS THAT DO NOT EXPAND

DEMANDED. Appendix G prints [ EXPANSION REQUIRED ] beside both.

  • AMMMS. Named beside BIST as maintenance messaging in the 2024 instruction manual. Never expanded, never defined, never mentioned again in four years of documents.
  • PHVDC. Used as an acronym everywhere. The corpus gives its behaviour — pulsed high-voltage DC carrying data and power on one pair, 300 V DC nominal, data 3 kHz / 1 Mbps, an homage to Teletype Model 19 / MIL-STD-1275 — and never its letters.

Also at K-15, because Appendix G has to print something either way.

H-07 · SHUTTING DOWN

DEMANDED. Upstream: holes report D-9.

Manual ¶5-17 exists as a position titled SHUTTING DOWN. No owner-facing power-off or sleep procedure exists anywhere in the corpus.

WHAT EXISTS AND IS NOT IT:

  • ¶7-19, written: "When the instrument is off, the power control alone remains live. No input is read, no sound is made, and no part of the display system is driven. An instrument that answers any other control while off is not off, and is to be withdrawn from service." — that is a test for whether it is off, not a procedure for turning it off.
  • The CRT lifecycle law (Mike, 2026-07-24): every screen-off is a CRT death — collapse to a horizontal line, then a point, then out. That is the presentation of an off, fleet-wide.
  • STORY_BIBLE.html: "Sleep-return (short) is the dominant wake pattern; reboot happens sometimes." — a statement about frequency, not a procedure.
  • The 65's SLEEP / screensaver is named in the porch capture and is not specced.

H-10 · THE DEGRADATION PAGE THE MACHINE POINTS AT

DEMANDED, and it is the only hole in this catalogue that the machine itself is built to point at. Upstream: holes report D-10.

H-11 · THE RUN-ACCESS PASSCODES ARE CARRIED AND CHECKED NOWHERE

UNASKED — recorded so it is not mistaken for a bug. Upstream: spec ledger #12.

MENU_PARAMETER_TABLE carries four passcode columns on every one of 187 rows. readAccess_ENABLED is honoured — 1 = visible, anything else = hidden. Run-access passcodes are carried in every row and checked nowhere in firmware.

The manual's own displayability doctrine (Appendix A-3) says a code, once accepted, is retained, and "a code may be accepted before its function exists and takes effect when the function arrives." That doctrine describes exactly this state, which makes it either a designed condition or a coincidence. Nobody has said which.

DEMANDED. Upstream: holes report C-8.

The manual's Appendix F carries three set names — system, density, ceremony — and Table 7-2 wants a FACE and a DUTY against each.

robots:docs/AESTHETIC_CANON-20260723.md §2.1 supplies both and calls it the font-role system: "Fonts never mix roles: the system speaks classic, density speaks TomThumb, ceremony speaks FreeSansBold."

H-13 · "DETERMINATION" IS A COINED WORD

UNASKED, and it is on a published page. Upstream: holes report C-9.

The manual calls the machine's output a determination, throughout, and in its glossary. The word appears in no source before the manual generator. It is a good word and it may well be the right one; it is also a coined term, which the DRAFTING LAW reserves to Mike ("agents assemble from established canon ONLY — no coined catchphrases, signatures, gags, or terminology. All flavor is Papa's.").

It is now published: ¶7-19's "Charging has no effect upon the determination" is on scan-07-a.

H-21 · THE SERIAL AND THE DATE ON THE FEED CONTROL'S BADGE

DEMANDED, and deliberately empty.

The nameplate carries three stamped fields. MODEL NO. reads TYPE 8p. SER. NO. and DATE render as struck wells with nothing in them — which is what an unstamped plate looks like, and which is deliberate: a plausible serial is exactly the failure the museum's Doctrine 12 names.

The day Mike hands over a serial it is one value, and it has a register row (P-a).

H-22 · THE ENGRAVED DRUM LEGENDS ARE OPS' WORDS

DEMANDED, flagged, unspent.

The engraved legends on the Portal's feed drum are Mike's to write. Ops cut STANDBY, COLD START, FIRST RUN, LAST STATE, TEST BENCH and DETAIL as period terms that fit the drum face at one line, and flagged them.

DETAIL is named on the register (CH-a) as Ops' word specifically.

And one legend is wrong by one word and is knowingly left alone: the drum's sub reads SELECT · ONE ARMED and two positions arm — standard (ch 3) and idling-updated (ch 4, DETAIL). Mike's ruling: fix it when the Portal is next touched, not in a round that is not about the Portal. It is the Record's own what's said matches what's shown rule applied to a control legend.

H-23 · THE TWIN HAS NO IN-FICTION HOME

UNASKED. MANUAL_STRUCTURE_FIT N-2 records it: the twin has no in-fiction home of any kind.

The public-twin doctrine gives it a frame — "you are running the original vintage software used to program and test MGK-VIIIp units" — and the museum's own FAQ gives it a description — "It is the real firmware on shimmed hardware — the twin. The unit itself is a physical object in a room; the twin is how it is met from here." Neither says where in the story the twin sits, who made it, or what it is a twin of in 1965 terms.

H-24 · WHERE THE STORY GOES IN THE MANUAL, IF ANYWHERE

DEMANDED. Upstream: holes report D 3; fit report §5.3.

An Operating and Maintenance Instructions is structurally hostile to sixty years of fiction, and the only precedent in the corpus is the 2022 proto-manual's one-line "Background" stub. If the story is meant to reach the reader in this document, the document type is wrong. Nobody has ruled it either way.

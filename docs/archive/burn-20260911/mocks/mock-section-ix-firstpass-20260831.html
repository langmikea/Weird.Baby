                                  SECTION IX

                               TROUBLESHOOTING


9-1.    GENERAL.

        Use this section when the instrument does not do what this manual says
        it does.

        The instrument reports its condition. It does not work out what is
        wrong with it. It cannot test itself, so it tells you the state it is
        in and stops there. Finding the cause is your job.

        Start with what the instrument showed. If it printed a report on the
        upper display, look that report up in Table 9-2. The table gives what
        it means and what to do.

        If the trouble is something you can see but the instrument has not
        reported, use the trouble chart at Table 9-1. It gives the fault, the
        likely cause, and the action.

        Run the maintenance routine at paragraph 8-7 before you treat anything
        as a fault. A good part of what is sent in for service needs nothing
        else.

        Not everything the instrument reports is a fault. A retry while
        starting, a determination it will not give again at once, no signal
        while a link is up, and a notice that the cell wants attention are all
        normal. Each is described where it belongs: paragraphs 5-13, 6-6, 7-14
        and 7-19.

        If this section does not put the instrument right, take it out of
        service and return it. Section XI says how.

9-3.    THE DIAGNOSTIC MONITOR AS A SERVICE AID.

        The diagnostic monitor is the resting face of the upper display and is
        described at paragraph 7-13. It is put to a second use here. What it
        shows while the instrument is worked is the readiest account of the
        instrument's state there is, and it is had without opening anything
        and without interrupting the operator.

        The glass is ruled into windows, each with a duty of its own. The
        window at the left shows the instrument's own working. The graph
        window, beside it, shows a measure taken over time, and shows signal
        in its place while a link is up; see paragraph 7-14. The window along
        the foot carries the process then running and the conditions the
        instrument reports.

        The window at the left does not stand at one thing. It goes between
        the working of the instrument, a readout of its present figures, and a
        prompt at which the instrument puts a short enquiry to itself and
        prints the reply. The enquiries are the instrument's own and are not
        put to it by the operator. They are read as they pass.

        The enquiries, and what each of them answers, are:

9-3

            STAT    the processes running, and the condition
            MEM     the state of what is compiled in
            SYNC    whether a link is up
            AGE     how often the instrument has been brought into service
            CHK     whether the condition warrants attention

        The monitor is a report and it is not a test. Nothing in it is run,
        and nothing in it is to be taken as a test having been performed; the
        instrument's tests are the matter of Section X. What the monitor shows
        is the instrument's own account of itself while it works, and it is
        worth reading before anything is taken apart.

        The conditions carried along the foot are given at paragraph 9-7 and
        in Table 9-2.

9-5.    SELF-CORRECTION.

        Self-correction during starting is given at paragraph 5-13 and is not
        repeated here. This paragraph is concerned with the same behaviour met
        in service.

        The instrument corrects itself in service as it does at starting. A
        step errors, is attempted again, and succeeds. Nothing is required of
        the operator and nothing is lost. The instrument reports that it has
        recovered, and carries how often it has done so with its other
        condition readings; see paragraph 8-9 and Table 8-2.

        The frequency is not fixed and it rides the condition of the
        instrument. One in good condition corrects itself seldom. One long in
        service without attention corrects itself often, and a frequency which
        is rising is the earliest notice there is of a condition falling. It
        is read as a trend and not as an event: a single recovery is not a
        fault reported.

        Where the instrument corrects itself repeatedly it does not leave the
        matter upon the glass. It sends the operator a message, which is held
        with the others and is dealt with at paragraph 6-23.

        Run the maintenance routine of paragraph 8-7 and read the condition
        again at paragraph 8-9. Where the frequency does not fall, the
        instrument is to be withdrawn from service.

9-7.    CONDITIONS REPORTED ON THE UPPER DISPLAY.

        The instrument reports its condition on the upper display, along the
        foot of the diagnostic monitor. The reports are given in Table 9-2
        with what each of them means and what each of them requires. A report
        states the instrument's state and carries no cause with it.

9-4

        The greater part of them state that the instrument is in order and are
        shown in the ordinary course of work. A report which is not carried in
        Table 9-2 is not a condition this section deals with.

        The parity bias setting mismatch is reported here. The communications
        settings are the matter of Appendix B, paragraph B-1. Where any one of
        the four disagrees with the far end the instrument does not open the
        link, and reports the disagreement. The report names the condition and
        does not name the setting at fault, there being no means at this end
        of knowing which of them is wrong.

        The four are therefore to be checked in turn against the record made
        at the time the link was established. What a disagreement in each of
        them does to the traffic is given in Table 9-1, that being the means
        of telling them apart.

        Where the instrument advises attention it advises it and does not
        withhold service. Attention advised is not a fault reported. Run the
        maintenance routine of paragraph 8-7.

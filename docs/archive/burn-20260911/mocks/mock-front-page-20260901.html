iv · Scope and arrangement

                     SCOPE AND ARRANGEMENT OF THIS MANUAL


    This is an ABEAL document and it is for use inside ABEAL. It is not
    supplied with the instrument and it is not given to a customer.

    It is preliminary. It is the company's own copy of the operating and
    maintenance instructions for the MGK-VIIIp, and the manual that goes
    out with the instrument has not been made.

    Read it as a working document. Where a section, paragraph, table or
    figure is numbered here and carries no text, the text has not been
    written. The position is held so that it does not move when the text
    is supplied.

5-3 · First run, established operation, external content transfer

5-5.    FIRST RUN.

        This sequence runs where the instrument finds a system record but no
        established user record. The instrument reports the record it found
        and says that it is setting up for the first time.

        It then makes three checks and reports each one as it passes: the
        memory, the audible system, and the display system. It reports them
        rather than making them silently, so that if you are there at first
        run you can see what it has satisfied itself of. A check that does not
        pass at the first attempt is dealt with at paragraph 5-13.

        The instrument then offers the external content transfer. This is the
        one point in any starting sequence at which it waits for you, and it
        is the only operator action in this section. The offer, what it
        carries and the two answers to it are given at paragraphs 5-9 to 5-11.

        When the offer has been answered the instrument completes the start
        and goes on to the menu. It does not run this sequence again.

5-7.    ESTABLISHED OPERATION.

        This sequence runs where the instrument finds a user record on file.
        The instrument reports the record, reports itself in order, and goes
        on to the menu.

        It is the shortest of the three and it asks nothing of you. An
        instrument in ordinary service runs this sequence at every start.

5-9.    EXTERNAL CONTENT TRANSFER.

        The instrument can take in content beyond what is compiled into it:
        messages, files, and the calibration of its response.

        It does not need this material in order to answer. An instrument that
        has received none of it is complete and in order.

        The transfer is offered rather than taken, and it is offered once, at
        first run. It is offered because it is made over your own arrangements
        and in your own time, and neither of those is the instrument's to
        assume.

        Neither answer is final. Content you do not take at first run stays
        available afterward, and the instrument tells you so at the time you
        postpone it.

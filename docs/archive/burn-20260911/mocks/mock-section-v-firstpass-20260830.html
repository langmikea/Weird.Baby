                                  SECTION V

                              STARTING PROCEDURE


5-1.    GENERAL.

        Starting is the procedure by which the instrument brings itself into
        service. It is not abbreviated and it is not omitted: an instrument
        brought up from cold starts in full, every time.

        Three starting sequences are provided. They are given in paragraphs
        5-3, 5-5 and 5-7 and are summarised in Table 5-1. The instrument
        determines which of the three it runs by consulting its own system
        record. The keeper does not select the sequence and cannot require
        one.

        An instrument in service does not start each time it is used. The
        short return from rest is the ordinary manner of waking and is not a
        starting sequence; a start proper is the less frequent of the two and
        occurs when the instrument is brought up from cold. What follows
        describes the start.

5-3.    FIRST INSTALLATION.

        The first installation sequence is run where the instrument finds no
        system record. The instrument reports the absence, investigates, and
        determines that a fresh installation is required.

        The installation is then taken down. The instrument declares its own
        estimate of the time required - EST 12 SECONDS - and reports the work
        as it proceeds. Nothing is asked of the keeper at any point in this
        sequence and no control need be touched.

        On completion the instrument does not go on to the menu. It restarts,
        and the start which follows finds a record where the first found none,
        so that the sequence of paragraph 5-5 is the one that then runs. An
        instrument which appears to start twice on first installation is
        behaving correctly.

        This sequence is run once in the life of an instrument. A keeper who
        meets it a second time is meeting an instrument whose system record
        has been lost.

5-5.    FIRST RUN.

        The first-run sequence is run where the instrument finds a system
        record but no established keeper record. The instrument reports the
        record found and states that it is setting up for the first time.

        Three checks are then made and each is reported as it passes: the
        memory, the audible system, and the display system. They are reported
        rather than made silently, so that a keeper present at first run sees
        what the instrument has satisfied itself of. A check which does not
        pass at the first attempt is dealt with at paragraph 5-13.

        The instrument then offers the external content transfer. This is the
        one point in any starting sequence at which the instrument waits for
        the keeper, and it is the only operator action in this section. The
        offer, what it carries and the two answers to it are given at
        paragraphs 5-9 to 5-11.

        When the offer has been answered the instrument completes the start
        and presents the menu. It does not run this sequence again.

5-7.    ESTABLISHED OPERATION.

        The established sequence is run where the instrument finds a keeper
        record on file. The instrument reports the record, reports itself in
        order, and presents the menu.

        It is the shortest of the three and it asks nothing. An instrument in
        ordinary service runs this sequence at every start.

5-9.    EXTERNAL CONTENT TRANSFER.

        The instrument is supplied able to receive content beyond that which
        is compiled into it: messages, files, and the calibration of its
        response. This material is not required for the instrument to answer,
        and an instrument which has received none of it is complete and in
        order.

        The transfer is offered rather than taken, and it is offered once, at
        first run. It is offered because it is made over the keeper's own
        arrangements and in the keeper's own time, neither of which is the
        instrument's to assume.

        Neither answer is final. Content not taken at first run remains
        available afterward, and the instrument says so at the time it is
        postponed.

5-4

    5-10.   Permitting the transfer.  The keeper permits the transfer by the
            shutter. The instrument reports that it is receiving, continues to
            report while the transfer is in hand, and completes the start when
            it is done.

    5-11.   Postponing the transfer.  The keeper postpones the transfer by the
            dial. The instrument reports the postponement, states that the
            content remains available, and completes the start.

            The offer does not stand indefinitely. It withdraws itself after
            two minutes and the instrument proceeds as though it had been
            postponed, so that an instrument left unattended at first run
            reaches the menu on its own. Postponement is therefore the answer
            the instrument takes where none is given.

5-13.   SELF-CORRECTION DURING STARTING.

        A step of a starting sequence may not succeed at the first attempt.
        Where this occurs the instrument reports that it is retrying, makes
        the attempt again, and goes on. A retry reported during starting is
        not a fault reported.

        The frequency with which it occurs is not fixed. It rides the
        condition of the instrument: one in good condition retries seldom, and
        one long in service without attention retries more often. The
        instrument counts its retries and carries the count with its other
        condition readings; see paragraph 8-9 and Table 8-2.

        Retries met in service rather than at starting are a maintenance
        matter and are dealt with at paragraph 9-5.

5-17.   SHUTTING DOWN.

        The instrument is shut down at the power control. It requires no
        preparation before the control is used.

        What remains live in the instrument when it is off, and the condition
        in which it is then to be left, are given at paragraph 7-19 and are
        not repeated here.

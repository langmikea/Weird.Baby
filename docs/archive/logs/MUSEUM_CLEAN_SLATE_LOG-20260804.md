## THE LAW, BANKED FIRST BECAUSE IT IS THE DELIVERABLE

> **If a line describes the work rather than doing the work, it does not ship.**

Written into `docs/canonical/OPERATIONS.md` §7 as Working Doctrine 11, with the
two adjacent traps that cost the most time this round (provenance is not meta;
mechanism state is not meta) and Mike's corollary, *empty and honest beats
populated and false*.

**The test is the line's SUBJECT, not its tone or its truth.** Every line
removed below was accurate. That is precisely what let it survive four rounds of
review: nobody could point at it and say it was wrong.

- **Fails** — the drafting, the research, the revision history, what a round
  did, the form a page takes and why, the typography, the renderers, the plan
  for unwritten content, any builder named on the glass, internal decision codes
  (`(C3)`), draft stamps (`· v1`).
- **Ships** — the objects, the artists, the events in the record, the standing
  terms, **and honest statements of what is not held.** "No plate on file" is a
  holdings fact. "Nobody has photographed this yet" is a production fact. Same
  absence, different subject.

## C2 — RECORD 013, STRIPPED FLAT

Mike's ruling taken literally, not split. **Same facts, same six sections, same
labels, same four doors, same marker positions, same headline, same order.**

**What came out:** the three-beat lists built for cadence ("newspaper, tape, and
the same brittle tan padding"); the quoted failure modes ("a smell we regret",
"the building has a bad afternoon"); the first-person colour ("a longer
conversation than it deserved to be", "the first time anyone here has seen this
thing alive"); the fragments used as beats ("The bag is wrong. Not
damaged-wrong — era-wrong."); and every sentence whose job was the turn at the
end of it.

One line came out as **meta rather than colour**: the still caption's "Nothing in
this entry has been photographed yet." A caption says what is in the frame, and
that sentence was about the museum's photography backlog. The caption still
names exactly what the plate shows, so it cannot be mistaken for the adapter.

Read on the built page, top to bottom. Two examples of the axis:

> **was** — "The bag is wrong. Not damaged-wrong — era-wrong. Three cartons of
> newsprint and tape and that tan padding that has gone to powder wherever it
> was touched, and then, taped flat against the inside wall of carton two, a
> modern sealed pouch…"
>
> **is** — "Taped flat against the inside wall of carton two was a sealed pouch:
> clear film, heat-crimped on all four edges, a machine-made seal. Inside it was
> one mains adapter."

> **was** — "The failure modes we sketched out run from “nothing happens”, which
> is the overwhelming favourite, through “a smell we regret”, to “the building
> has a bad afternoon”."
>
> **is** — "The failure modes considered ran from nothing happening, which is the
> most likely outcome, through smoke, to a fire."

**Verified on glass:** dateline `17 JAN 24 · WEEK 3 · WEDNESDAY · RECORD 013`

## C3 — PLACEHOLDER CONTENT, SITE-WIDE

### Thirteen invented visitors (`HrExhibitFlow.jsx`)

`SEED_ENTRIES` held thirteen fabricated journal entries — invented handles
(`velvetcassette`, `nick_would_know`), invented dates, invented vote counts, and
invented first-person testimony about a real living musician and his dead
brother: *"I lost my brother two years ago. This song found me at exactly the
right time."*

**Stated accurately because the measurement was taken: this was NOT reachable.**
`JournalContent` renders only when `activeTab === "journal"`; the tab strip
filters that key out (BAR-DOCK, 2026-06-16); the two surviving player-bar
triggers are FILTERS and PRESETS; `handleTabClick` is the only setter. Verified
on the built page. **No visitor has been reading these.** Removed anyway — a
fabricated testimonial in the tree is one re-enabled tab away from being a
fabricated testimonial on the page. A mojibake em-dash (`ΓÇö`) in two of them
died with the block. Honest empty state added.

### `/hr/home` — an annotated wireframe at a live address

**The find of the round, and it came from a screenshot rather than a grep.** The
page's own DOM text was three words: `HUNTER ROOT · HOMESTEAD`. Everything else
a visitor read there — **MUSEUM MENU · SPECIAL ON ROTATION · POSTERS / FAN ART ·
MEDIA ROOM · ARCHIVES (downstairs) · EXIT · BULLITEN (sic) · HR** — was **marker
lettering painted into `public/museum.jpg`**, a 1024×680 stock interior
photograph. A design mockup, shipped as a room, advertising five rooms of which
four have never existed in this application, and misspelling one of them.

No text sweep could ever have caught it. **Component and asset both deleted.**

## C4 — WHAT THE REMOVAL EXPOSES

Listed plainly. Nothing below is papered over, and none of it was created by
this round — it was **covered by** the things this round removed.

### Rooms that are now empty or nearly so

1. **`/hr/home` no longer exists.** The Hunter Root wing's "homestead" landing is
   gone. `/hr` (the real exhibit, deck and all) is untouched and unaffected.
   **`/hr/archive` is now reachable only by typing its URL** — its own way back
   already pointed at `/hr`, so nothing dangles, but nothing points *in* either.
2. **The Hunter Root wing has one page and one list.** `/hr` and `/hr/archive`.
   The four rooms the mockup advertised — a media room, a fan wall, a workshop,
   a lyric map — do not exist and never did. The wing is unlisted on the lobby
   board and now has no lobby of its own.
3. **The `/hr` journal is dead machinery.** With the fakes gone, the honest state
   is visible: the tab is unreachable, and `submitEntry` pushes to component
   state and nothing else — **an entry a visitor left would survive until the
   page reloaded and no further.** The lobby's guest book is a real D1 table;
   this is not one. *Removing the fake entries did not make the box honest — it
   made what the box does visible.* Building or retiring it is a feature
   decision and yours.
4. **`/wb` is thin and always was.** Six audio tracks, no faces, no artifact
   deck, no facts (`facts: []`, `exhibitFlow` omitted). Nothing was removed from
   it. Naming it because it is now among the thinnest things on the board.
5. **`/shop` carries one object.** A sticker, plus doors to the artists' own
   stores. The room says so itself, so it is honest — but it is one shelf.

### Content gaps the fact vault removal exposes

6. **Jesse Welles' "That Can't Be Right" now has ZERO song-tier facts.** All
   three it had were our research narrative. Its pop-up climbs to the artist
   tier, which is the documented behaviour of the climb and not a fault — but
   **this museum holds almost nothing about that song that is about the song.**
   Its card is now one sentence, and one sentence is what we can honestly say.
7. **"There's A Hole" drops from two song facts to one.** The file's own note
   says a song with one fact reads as a scroller that is stuck.
8. **Nothing was invented to fill either hole.** That was deliberate and it is
   the part that needs your ruling: these two want real facts, or they want the
   thin card they now have.

### Faces still standing on empty containers

9. **The Manual's reel is empty and now says so in four words** ("No pages on
   file"), where before it explained at length why. The container is built; the
   photographs are unmade. Unchanged by this round, more visible after it.
10. **The Record is one written entry and ten one-line index rows.** The ten
    were kept — real dates and real events from the actual archive, not
    invented — but they are index rows, not entries, and the footer no longer
    dresses that up as a plan. **Entry two is still the real test of the form.**
11. **Sixty entries against a wing that owns eight photographs.** Carried from
    v45 and untouched.

# THE FOUNDATION COPY — round log (v54; F1–F8)

**2026-08-05. Single agent, drafting lane. Sealed.**

Mike answered the Foundation's questions himself. Every round before this one
built that room by **digging** — reading the charter, the raw dictations, the
booth, the robots repo, and assembling answers out of things he had already said
somewhere else. This round he sat down and answered them.

The instruction that came with the answers is the only one that governs this log:

> **LAND HIS PHRASING. Do not improve it. Do not add a fact he did not give.
> Where he wrote an INSTRUCTION rather than copy, honour the instruction and
> MARK the gap — never fill it.**

**So the interesting work was almost all subtraction and marking.** Two questions
were deleted, one answer stopped publishing entirely, two real households did not
reach the page, and four gaps are now marked and empty where a plausible sentence
would have fitted. What arrived is smaller than what left, and that is the round.

## F1 — his copy, question by question

### "Why give all the money away?" — Motto #3 leads, and one half is missing

He ruled that this answer **carries Motto #3** — *Take all you need, not grab all
you can* — and that it also needs the content about him and his household
already having enough, **which he flagged as still to come.**

The motto was the third sentence of the paragraph and is now the first. That is
the whole of the edit to the existing prose: it was buried behind a lead-in whose
only job was to arrive at it, and nothing else in the approved answer was
contradicted, so nothing else moved.

**The motto ships as words, not as a number.** He calls it Motto #3. This museum
has never published a numbered list of mottos, so printing *"motto number three"*
would name an apparatus a visitor has never seen — an internal designation on the
glass, which is Doctrine 11's own example of a line that fails. Recorded as
[M40](OPEN_ACTIONS.md#m40) so it is not re-argued.

**The Mike-and-Mo half is a marker and not a paraphrase.** Nothing plausible was
written into it. On the one page in this building whose entire subject is honesty
about money, a stand-in is the exact failure Doctrine 12 exists for.

### "Where does it actually go?" — a pointer, and the round's one judgement call

His answer was an **instruction rather than copy**: *the ledger, not prose.*

**Honoured literally it would have pointed at the wrong half.** The register on
that page is the **incoming** side — five channels through which money and goods
arrive. *"Read the ledger"* in answer to *where does it go* sends a reader to a
table that does not contain the answer, and a pointer that points at the wrong
thing is worse than the paragraph it replaced.

So the call, and it is flagged as a call rather than buried as a decision:

- The charter's own beneficiary clause **stayed** as the pointer's destination in
  words — *"To established charitable organisations, and to people caught doing
  conspicuous good."* It is settled law, already published, and deleting a
  sourced fact was never what *make it a pointer* asked for.
- The rest of the paragraph **went**.
- The answer now names the ledger and declares **the outgoing half NOT BUILT**,
  in the room's own grammar. That is mechanism state, which Doctrine 11 names
  explicitly as the permitted case — not a note about a backlog.

**What was deleted is not lost.** The application-and-grant-form argument went
with the paragraph, and he re-supplied it himself, in better words, as the
Pro-Tip on the very next question. The alternative — a bare *"Read the ledger."*
with the charter clause gone too — is one string, and it is
[M43](OPEN_ACTIONS.md#m43).

## F2 — the two deletions, and only one of them was free

**"So who is actually paying for all this?" — DROPPED, and nothing went with it.**
The question existed to carry the posture. The posture is still on the page,
signed, under the object it explains. The reasoning it originally shipped with —
*that sentence is worth landing twice, once as a signature and once as a
question* — is precisely the argument Doctrine 16 was written against: a second
object saying what the first already said, defended on the grounds that the
sentence is good. The sentence is good. It did not need to be there twice.

**"Who pays you?" — DELETED, and this one cost the page something.** Recorded
rather than absorbed:

- the only enumeration in the building of the charter's four gifts of service —
  **the design, the code, the shelf, the legal work**;
- the only sentence stating the job pays *nothing: not salary, not expenses, not
  credit taken.*

The **substance** survives twice over — the invoice IS four costs billed at
nothing, and the posture says who carries them and for how long. The **list** does
not. Worth flagging because this exact clause has moved twice already: v41/C3
removed the legal-work item, v42/R3 restored it on a careful argument, and this
round removed the whole list. **That argument is not being re-run — his ruling
stands.** [M42](OPEN_ACTIONS.md#m42) exists so that if the four gifts should stand
somewhere else, it is a decision he makes rather than a thing that vanished.

## F3 — the billionaires answer stops publishing

His ruling came in three parts: **the raw ideas are good, the voice is wrong, the
placement is wrong.**

So every sentence of the answer now carries the marker. `visitorProse` returns an
empty string, the `kept` filter drops the whole entry, and **`/foundation` no
longer asks about billionaires at all.** It is the first answer in this building
to take that path all the way down — the mechanism has existed since P5 and had
only ever eaten single sentences.

**Not one word reaches a visitor in Ops voice**, which is what *the voice is
wrong* has to mean if it means anything. An answer he has told us does not sound
like him is not improved by being softened; it comes down until he writes it.

**The three ideas are preserved verbatim** as his raw material: the *Illionaires*
coinage, *being remembered for the size of the pile is a strange thing to spend a
life earning*, and *we would rather make more pie than fight over the last piece.*

**One sentence was deliberately not carried forward.** *"Why keep grabbing for
more?"* is the one line in the answer with no idea of his under it — a rhetorical
question Ops added to close the paragraph. Putting Ops rhetoric into the pile he
is being asked to write from would defeat the point of holding it.

**The placement is still open and nothing was moved.** He questioned whether this
belongs in this room at all; choosing the room would be Ops answering the question
he asked. [M41](OPEN_ACTIONS.md#m41).

## F5 — the rule published; the names did not

His supply had two halves and they went different ways.

**The RULE shipped**, as the money section's law, set the same way *"We list only
what we need"* is set — it governs every row above it rather than describing any
one of them:

> Money given for a cost is spent promptly on that cost, or it goes back.
> Nothing is pooled and there is no slush fund.

**The MECHANISM shipped**, as one sentence in the invoice's small print: *a cost
carried by somebody other than the keeper takes its own line here, in the name of
whoever carried it.* It is a standing rule of that document and is true before it
has an instance, exactly as the register's NOT BUILT rows are true before they are
built.

**The two named lines did not ship, and this is the one item in the round Ops
refused.** He supplied two zero-cost invoice lines with real people against real
costs — one household against the **domain**, another against the **stickers**.
They are the strongest rows that object could carry: a named person carrying a
named cost is the room's whole argument drawn rather than asserted.

They are held because **publishing a private individual's name on a public page is
not the museum's call to make on their behalf**, and this house has already taken
a name off the glass once for less — CS (2026-08-04) removed the operator's *own*
name from a sentence that did not need it. These are other people's names, which
is a higher bar, not a lower one.

**The names appear nowhere in this repository** — not in `src/`, and deliberately
not in this log either, since it is committed. They were given to Mike directly in
the round's report. What is needed is one word per household.
[M38](OPEN_ACTIONS.md#m38).

## F6 — two doors, named, with no addresses

He renamed both questions to his own titles — *"Can I send money?"* → **"Can I
donate?"**, *"Can I send you something you need?"* → **"Can I send you
something?"** — and marked a link on each without supplying either address.

Built as instructed and **left visibly unresolved**: each renders as the door's
name in the register face with the ledger's own `NOT BUILT` stamp beside it.

**There is no `<a>` element at all**, and that is the decision in it. An anchor
with no `href`, or one pointing at `#`, is a dead control, and Doctrine 11's
corollary says a dead control is removed rather than left standing. What ships is
the door's **name** and its **state** — nothing to click, nothing to be
disappointed by.

**The state is read from the reveal ledger** (`channel.qr`, `channel.supplies`),
not hard-coded. On the day either channel is built the stamp flips without this
file being edited, which is the whole reason for wiring it rather than typing a
chip. Measured at 373px: both slots wrap to two lines cleanly, zero overflow.

[M39](OPEN_ACTIONS.md#m39) holds the two addresses.

## F7 — the directory indents one entry

Mike's note. *Other Music Worth a Listen* now sits one step in from the other
entries on the lobby board.

It is the only entry beginning with the word **Other**, and the word was doing
work the flush-left list flattened: the first two lines are the house's own, this
one is everybody else's. M8 built the ORDER to say that and left both kinds on the
same margin. An indent is the plainest way a board says *and also these*.

**The padding is on the label, not the button** — the button owns the row's rule
and holds the arrow against the right edge, so indenting it would pull the
hairline in and break the column of arrows.

**Measured, because the row is `nowrap` and this is the longest label on the
board** — a `nowrap` row that outgrows its box does not wrap, it overhangs. At the
tightest width the lap could produce (a 373px content column, **tighter than a
real 390px phone**): the entry's content box is **256px** and the indented label
plus arrow needs **240px**. It fits, with **sixteen pixels of slack**. At a true
390px the box is 270px and the slack is 30px.

**Sixteen pixels is recorded rather than rounded up to "fine."** A first pass of
this comment asserted *"234px against a 200px label, room to spare"* — numbers
written before the measurement and wrong in both directions. The lap caught it and
the comment now carries what was actually measured.

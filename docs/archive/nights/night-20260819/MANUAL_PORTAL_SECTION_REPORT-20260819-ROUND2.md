## Q1 — THE PORTAL IS SOFTWARE. DOES THE NINE-POSITION SHAPE HOLD?

**Answer: two thirds of it holds, one third is struck, and the ruling improves
the section rather than damaging it. Section IV is out. Section VI is right —
in fact it is where every other piece of software in this machine already
lives.**

### 1.1 What the manual already does with anything non-physical — which is most of it

Ops went looking for the manual's precedent and found that the question has been
answered on nearly every page. **This document is already more than half a
software manual, and it has never once used the word.**

- **Sections V, VI and X are software end to end** — the three starting
  sequences, the whole menu system and every answer family, and BIST / checksum
  / userdata / code runner / SCAT. **That is 33 of the 107 paragraph
  positions.**
- **Five of the eight appendices are software schedules** — A access codes,
  B bias settings, C the engine schedule, D the menu index, E voices.
- **Most of Section VII and Section IX** are software too: the prediction and
  answer engines, the response curve, the three biases, the video link, the
  character sets, retention of the record, self-correction, and the whole of
  Table 9-2 REPORTED CONDITIONS, which is literally program output.
- **The physical manual is Sections II, III, IV, VIII (in part), XI and XII.**
  Six of twenty divisions.

**AND IT HAS A COMPLETE VOCABULARY FOR SOFTWARE, WHICH IS NOT THE WORD
"SOFTWARE".** The verb is **FITTED** — 6-29 FUNCTIONS NOT FITTED, Table C-2
ENGINE POSITIONS NOT FITTED IN INSTRUMENTS OF GENERAL ISSUE, 1-9 CONFIGURATIONS
SUPPLIED. The nouns are **FUNCTION**, **PROGRAM** (6-21 ANCILLARY PROGRAMS, and
PROGRAMS is one of the four doors), **POSITION**, **ENGINE**, **CODE**. The one
place *install* appears is C-3 PERSONALITY INSTALLATION. So a software thing in
this document **is fitted or not fitted, occupies a position, and is worked as a
function.** Nothing needs inventing and nothing needs the modern word.

**AND THERE IS ALREADY A POSITION FOR SOFTWARE IDENTITY.** 1-5 INSTRUMENT
IDENTIFICATION has two subordinates: SP 1-6 *Serial designation* and
**SP 1-7 *Compile date and issue*.** *Compile date* is the corpus's own word for
a software version — the engines carry compile dates 1945 / 1946 / 1965. **So if
`TYPE 8p` is to appear in this manual at all, SP 1-7 is its position** — not a
nameplate row in Section XII, and not Table 12-2's EQUIPMENT / CATALOG NO.

### 1.2 Section IV is struck, and the manual's own precedent is what strikes it

**Table 4-1's first column is `INDEX NO.`, and it is a call-out number keyed to
Figure 4-1 — "CONTROLS AND INDICATORS, FORWARD AND UPPER", a labelled drawing of
the two faces.** A software control has no index number, because it is not on a
face. There is nowhere in that table for it to go.

**And the manual has already had this exact case and solved it elsewhere.** The
bias settings are software controls — worked with the dial and the shutter,
drawn on the front glass. Their procedure is **Appendix B-3 ENABLE, SET,
ADJUST**, and their values are **Tables B-1, B-2, B-3**. They are not in Section
IV and never were. **Section IV has never contained a software control.** So the
four table-row sets in the round-1 proposal become three: the Table 4-1 rows are
withdrawn.

### 1.3 The harder question the ruling exposes — and it is Mike's

My round-1 question 2 was *whose equipment is the FEED CONTROL?* The ruling
dissolves it and puts a better one in its place.

**WHO IS THE READER STANDING AT?** This manual is the MGK-VIIIp's. Its reader is
the instrument's keeper. But SP 7-14 — already written — says the far end **"is
a station in its own right and is drawn on its own glass."** The FEED CONTROL is
software with its own badge, and the museum visitor who reads this manual is
standing **at the far end**, at that control, looking through the bezel.

So there are two readings and they give sections of very different sizes:

**READING A — the FEED CONTROL is the instrument's own program.** The operator
selects which far end the upper display links to. The round-1 run of paragraphs
is right, Table 6-3 FEED POSITIONS is right, and it belongs in an appendix
beside the other schedules.

**READING B — the FEED CONTROL is the far end's program.** Then the VIIIp's
manual documents it the way a period manual documents anything at the far end:
**not at all, except as a condition to be satisfied** — which SP 7-14 already
does, completely. The drum, the channels, the source dial and the latch are
another station's controls and are none of this manual's business. **Table 6-3
falls with them.**

**Ops reads the ruling as pointing at B**, because *"the Feed Ctrl is SW"* plus
*"the far end is a station in its own right"* puts the control at the station,
not on the instrument. **But that is an inference on one clause and Ops will not
make it.** It is the load-bearing question and it decides whether the section is
six paragraphs or two.

### 1.5 One thing found while re-examining, and it changes what this section is FOR

`docs/OPEN_ACTIONS.md` row **PZ-a**: *Record 1.5 has the Portal **fail to boot on
a Communications Parity Bias Setting Mismatch**, with **four unlabelled toggles
under a guard**.* Two proposed answers were rejected — one does not scale past a
single channel, the other gates on outside knowledge (*"going to scare away
anybodies"*). The constraint is that it **must be solvable by someone who has
only read the museum.**

**Those four toggles are Appendix B-1's four settings.** PARITY · DUPLEX · WORD ·
STOP, and the written paragraph already says *"the unit will not speak until all
four agree with the far end"* and *"the values are not given in this manual. They
are established at the far end."*

Three consequences:

1. **The Portal section is not decorative. It is where "solvable by someone who
   has only read the museum" comes from.** The manual is the museum's readable

2. **It also answers the boot-that-fails question Ops carried forward.** The
   failure already exists in the story, at a named condition, with a written
   appendix behind it. What does not exist is the mechanism (PZ-a, open, *Mike +

3. **AND IT IS A HAZARD FOR THIS SECTION.** Writing 6-33's procedure without

   established at the far end — which is period-correct, is what the written

**Proposal not rewritten beyond the strikes above, per instruction. §1.3 is the
question that decides the rest.**

## Q2 — PASTED, APPLIED TO THE PAGE

**Ruling understood, and the good news is that the mechanism is already shaped
for it. Nothing has to change to make the print grey with the page — that is
where `draw_paste` already writes.**

Measured in `render_page`, the order is:

1. `("p", …)` ops call `draw_paste(paper, …)` — **into the PAPER layer**, not the ink layer.
2. Only then does the camera-and-copier chain run over the whole sheet:
   `out = paper − ink·field·(paper−6)` → Gaussian blur 0.75 → the copier's
   S-curve `v + (v²(3−2v) − v)·0.8` → a second low-frequency field → toner dirt
   and gutter dust → Gaussian noise σ1.1 → feed skew → scan skew → the platen
   band.

**So anything inside the paste box takes every one of those passes, exactly as
the typed characters do.** It halftones, it greys, it picks up the same dirt, it
rotates with the sheet. Mike's ruling is already the mechanism's shape.

**And the applied-object cues are already there too**, all deterministic from
`SEED`: a wobbling scissor cut on all four edges (`hand_line`, wob 0.9), a border
at value 132, a blurred alpha shadow at value 206 offset +4 px right and +5 px
down **pasted before the print**, and a tilt — one figure in six gets ±0.9–1.1°,
the rest ±0.17°.

**What to build, then, is only this:** the image goes into the box *before* the
cut edge and the border are drawn over it, so the cut still reads as a cut
through the print rather than a frame around it; the shadow and tilt are
untouched; and nothing in the copier chain is bypassed for it. **A clean
rectangle of image with the edge treatment dropped would be the failure mode —
that is printed-on, and it is what the ruling excludes.**

## Q3 — WHAT THE GENERATOR NEEDS FROM AN IMAGE

### 3.1 The frame, measured

The frame is **always 68 of the 78 columns wide** (`col0 = 5, ncols = COLS − 10`)
and its height quantises to whole 40-px lines.

| | |
|---|---|
| Width, master (240 dpi) | **1360 px** = 5.667 in = **143.9 mm** — fixed, every figure |
| Height | `rows × 40 px`, where `rows = round(h_mm ÷ 4.2333)` |
| Tallest that fits a body page | **44 rows = 1760 px** (48 body rows, less 4 for blanks and caption) |
| Width in the 300-dpi page raster | **1700 px** |
| Width in what a visitor downloads (museum 1700×2200 WebP) | **≈1133 px** |

Achievable aspects (width ÷ height), with what each fits:

| rows | px tall | aspect | fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 640 | 2.125 | — |
| 17 | 680 | 2.000 | Figures 7-2, 7-3 today |
| 19 | 760 | **1.789** | **16:9 — a browser screenshot** |
| 20 | 800 | 1.700 | Figures 5-1, 6-1 |
| 21 | 840 | 1.619 | 16:10 |
| 22 | 880 | 1.545 | Figure 1-1 |
| 23 | 920 | 1.478 | 3:2; Figures 4-1, 7-1, A-1 |
| 27 | 1080 | 1.259 | **the twin's own fixed 1.25 frame** |

**Aspect is not the problem.** 16:9 wants 765 px and the grid gives 760 — a 0.65 %
squash, invisible. The twin's 1.25 frame lands on 27 rows, 0.7 % off.

### 3.2 What to send

- **Format:** PNG or WebP, RGB or RGBA. It is `.convert("L")`'d at the door, so
  colour is discarded there — **flatten alpha onto white before sending** or the
  transparent corners come through black. Keep the colour master (Article 4).
- **Width: ≥ 2720 px** — 2× the master frame. At `devicePixelRatio` 2 that is a
  1360 CSS-px viewport. Below 1360 px the frame is *upscaling*, which is the one
  thing that cannot be recovered.
- **Aspect:** anything from about 1.25 to 2.1 lands on a frame that looks like
  the ten already in the book. 16:9 is the best fit.
- **Crop to the control, not to the browser.** No browser chrome, no page
  background. The frame is a hand-cut print and a cut lands on a joint or a
  panel edge — the obfuscation law's Article 2 reads across to a composition
  even when the subject is software.

### 3.3 How a screenshot differs from a photograph on this page — three real differences

**(1) THE SMALLEST FEATURE, AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT WILL BITE.** A photograph of
the machine has no feature finer than a screw head. **A screenshot of the Portal
is made almost entirely of fine features** — 1-px rules, engraved legends at
roughly 10–12 px, and a nameplate whose whole effect is two directions of relief
at small scale. Everything in Q2's chain then runs over it: blur 0.75, an S-curve
that pushes midtones apart, two low-frequency fields, dirt, σ1.1 noise, and two
BICUBIC rotations. **1-px lines and 11-px type do not survive that.** The cure is
either a tight crop (one control at large scale, not the whole panel) or the 2×
capture, and preferably both.

**(2) TONE. The panel is dark and the page is white.** `draw_paste` lays its
print at value 244 on paper 252, and the copier chain is built for mostly-light
content. A near-black 1360 × 760 rectangle would be **the darkest object in the
whole 63-page document**, and the S-curve crushes it toward a flat slab with a
few white marks. **That may be exactly right** — a 1965 office copier reproducing
a photograph of a lit screen gives you precisely that, and it is a good look —
**but it has to be chosen, not discovered.** The alternative is to lift the black
point before pasting so the panel reads as dark grey rather than as a hole.

**(3) THE ASPECT MUST COME FROM THE FILE.** Today the author declares `h_mm` and
`rows` is computed from it, so **the frame's shape is chosen before the image
exists.** With a real image that inverts: `rows = round(1360 ÷ (w/h) ÷ 40)`, and
`h_mm` becomes derived. This is the museum's own 2026-08-17 lesson — an
`aspect-ratio` picked before the files were measured would have cropped 44 % off
two portrait objects.

IT HOLDS FOR THIS SOURCE, NOT FOR EVERY VIDEO. The crop is
unconditional - 12.5% of the player's width a side, and only 70.15% of
the frame inside the hole at all. Here that is entirely matte. A
genuine 16:9 clip would lose about 30% of its picture to the same rule
with nothing reporting it, because no gate in this tree can read a
cross-origin frame's pixels. That is a property of the source, not of
the placement, and it is filed at the swap site beside ytId and as row
T-a.

The captions ruling needed two mechanisms and only one binds.
cc_load_policy: 0 is the weak half - YouTube documents 1 as force-on
and reads anything else as the viewer's own preference. The half that
does not depend on the viewer is unloadModule("captions"), which needed
a three-line passthrough on useYTPlayer that /hr and /wal never call.
It runs on its own 500ms timer rather than in the refusal watcher,
because that one stops the moment the set is playing and the module
comes back on every loadVideoById - once every 1,743 seconds on the
loop. Verified on a browser that had captions on.

provenance:gate failed on "captions" and "cc" and was right to: it is
default-DENY. Declaring a YouTube module name as museum content would
have been the wrong shape, so unloadModule joins SINK_ALL, the sweep's
named list of call targets whose string arguments are machinery, beside
postMessage and addEventListener. One entry, not a family - the sweep's
own rule is that an over-broad look-away is the defect. The look-away
is counted: machinery-call reads 218, up 2.

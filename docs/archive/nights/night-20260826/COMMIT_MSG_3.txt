The centre 4:3 behind the bezel, and read records dim instead of unread ones

Two packets land together because the tree carries both and only the session
notes overlap.

THE CROP. The aperture cannot be made 4:3 and that is what decides the reading:
the opening measures 2532 by 2003, which is 1.264, on a plate whose replacement
is shelved. So the centre 4:3 area is a fact about the picture, and the
instruction means what it says. The three candidates the survey offered were
three answers to which rectangle counts as the screen, and each placement
already knows its own, so the rule is written once: the canvas takes 3000 by
2400 to 3200 by 2400 at x -100, which enlarges the plates by exactly sixteen
fifteenths, and the feed takes 2540 by 2036 to 2714.67 by 2036. Both are the
centre 4:3 that covers the rect rather than one inscribed in it, since
inscribing letterboxes and that is the opposite of enlarging. The measured
opening is deliberately unused: the feed rect is taller than the hole on purpose
so no ground can leak in, and cropping to the hole's own bounding box would hand
that back to buy thirteen pixels. It takes a sixth off television's black and
does not close it, 303.6 pixels a side down to 254.5, because object-fit is
inert on an iframe; closing it means sizing the player to the box's height, and
that is a separate job and is not built.

THE RESIZE. Shape B was reported and neither shape was built, because B is about
returning to channel 3 and the resize happens on the first open too. Measured by
construction instead: removing the injected framedfit rule from a live framed
twin gives 828 by 662 at x 36 against 900 by 720 at x 0, an 8.7 per cent growth
and a 36 pixel shift, scheduled by a network fetch. Framed_Fit now runs in setup
before Unit_LoadPhotos; nothing in it ever needed the probe, and setup runs from
an inline script at the end of the body. It also fixes the missing-base-plate
case, where framed styling had never run at all. So there is nothing left to
cover, and a cover would have been Mike's word rather than Ops'.

THE MARKING. The rule down the left of every unread row is struck and the
polarity inverts: a read record has its number and day slightly dimmed. It
marked the wrong state, and the cost landed on the visitor who matters most --
a first-time reader has read nothing, so every row grew a rule, loudest exactly
when it carried no information. The original comment's argument survives the
flip word for word, because scanning sixty rows was never an argument about
polarity. The rule was not this week's work: it dates from 8fe959f on 5 August
and the unread path has been untouched since the 19th; it became visible because
the read set is localStorage per origin.

Slightly is 0.86, and it is the palette's floor rather than a taste. The first
figures were computed against a ground that is not there -- vp-face declares
--wb-ink but the first ancestor that actually paints is vp-face-body at #faf8f3
-- which made every number wrong in the safe direction. On what really paints,
the number reads 7.12 to 1 and the day 6.30; at 0.86 they are 5.01 and 4.53 and
both still AA, and at 0.85 the day drops under. The day binds because
--wb-gold-mute was raised specifically to clear 4.5 to 1 on this class of small
mono, and spending that ruling is not Ops' to do. A stronger dim is available at
0.80 and it is Mike's call.

read is a second prop rather than the absence of unread, because unread carries
a list-length guard that is about the list and not the row, and styling the
negation would have dimmed the single row the dictation preview renders -- the
row Mike writes into.

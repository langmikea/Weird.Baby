The centre 4:3 behind the bezel, and the resize that needed no cover

The aperture cannot be made 4:3 and that is what decides the reading. The
opening measures 2532 by 2003, which is 1.264, and it is barrel-curved art on a
plate whose replacement is shelved, so no amount of code makes the visible hole
1.333. The centre 4:3 area is therefore a fact about the picture, and the
instruction means what it says: enlarge what is behind until the hole frames the
middle of it.

The three candidates the survey offered were three answers to which rectangle
counts as the screen, and each placement already knows its own, so the rule is
written once. The canvas placement takes 3000 by 2400 to 3200 by 2400 at x -100,
which enlarges the plates by exactly sixteen fifteenths; the feed placement takes
2540 by 2036 to 2714.67 by 2036. Both are the centre 4:3 that covers the rect
rather than one inscribed in it, because inscribing letterboxes and that is the
opposite of enlarging, and the object-fit already declared does the rest. The
measured opening is the one deliberately not used: the feed rect is taller than
the hole on purpose so no page ground can leak in, and cropping to the hole's own
bounding box would hand that guarantee back to buy thirteen pixels.

It takes a sixth off television's black and does not close it -- 303.6 pixels a
side down to 254.5, thirty per cent of the opening down to twenty-five. The root
is the same one that put channel 4 in an iframe: object-fit is inert on one, so a
wider box only changes how the player letterboxes inside it. Closing it means
sizing the player to the box's height and overflowing its width, which is a
change to how television is placed rather than to the crop, and it is not built.

Shape B was reported and neither shape was built, because B would not have fixed
what was reported. B keeps the twin mounted so returning to channel 3 costs no
reload, and the resize happens on the first open too. The cause was measured by
construction instead: removing the injected framedfit rule from a live framed
twin gives 828 by 662 at x 36 against 900 by 720 at x 0, an 8.7 per cent growth
and a 36 pixel shift, scheduled by a network fetch. Framed_Fit now runs in setup
before Unit_LoadPhotos. Nothing in it ever needed the probe -- it adds a class
and injects CSS, and Framed is a synchronous window test -- and setup runs from
an inline script at the end of the body, so it lands before the stage is painted
once. It also fixes the missing-base-plate case, where framed styling had never
run at all and the NO SIGNAL card drew in the small centred stage.

So there is nothing left to cover. A cover would have been a thing a visitor
looks at and therefore Mike's word rather than Ops', but the resize is gone
rather than hidden and what remains is a load, which is what every channel does.

First-paint timing could not be measured and the probe says so rather than
guessing: the pane does not composite, a 25ms sampler returned five samples
across 4,682ms, and the twin's own paint entries come back empty. The round log
is at docs/MUSEUM_CROP_AND_COVER_LOG-20260826.md and nothing was deployed.

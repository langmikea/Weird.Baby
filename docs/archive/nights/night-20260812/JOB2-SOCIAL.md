## 2a. PLATFORMS

**Recommendation: two posting accounts, one held name. Not three posting accounts.**

| Brand | Platform | Posts? |
|---|---|---|
| **Weird.Baby Robots** | **Instagram** (own account) | yes |
| **Weird.Baby Music** | **YouTube** (own channel) + the same video cross-posted to **Instagram** (own account) | yes |
| **Weird.Baby** (the house) | name registered everywhere, bio links the site | **no — holds only** |
| Worth A Listen | posts from the **Music** account. No account of its own. | — |
| Foundation | nothing. No account, no mention, no bio line. | never |

**PLAINLY: yes, Robots and Music go on SEPARATE ACCOUNTS.** Not for branding —
for mechanics. Instagram's free native scheduler is per-account, so two accounts
means two independent queues you can fill weeks apart. And an account's *name* is
the only label a viewer reads before deciding to follow; a feed that alternates a
machine photograph with a song trains neither audience to expect anything.

**PLAINLY: Weird.Baby itself does NOT post.** It registers the name on every
platform on day one, puts `weird.baby` in the bio, posts once ("the museum is
open"), and then goes quiet for ninety days. That is a **hold**, not an account —
it exists so nobody else takes the name and so a person who searches the brand
finds something that points at the site. Three posting accounts is three times
the work and you are one person.

**Noise — do not post to these:**

- **Bluesky** — has **no native scheduling at all** (see 2d). Every post is a
  manual daily action. That breaks the alone-for-90-days constraint outright.
- **TikTok** — native scheduling caps at **10 days** and is desktop-only, so you
  must sit down every 10 days for 90 days. Also video-only, and Robots has no
  video assets.
- **X / Twitter** — native scheduler is single posts only, no threads, desktop
  only. Low return for a serialized fiction.
- **Threads** — could not verify whether native scheduling exists (see 2d). Do
  not build a cadence on an unverified mechanic.
- **Facebook Page** — a Page is only worth it if you want Meta Business Suite as
  your scheduling desk. Instagram's in-app scheduler already covers you, so skip.
- **Substack / Discord / Reddit** — Substack in particular would be a second
  publication competing with the site. The site **is** the publication.

Register the name on the noise platforms anyway (2d). Registering is not posting.

## 2b. FORMAT — one recommendation each

**ROBOTS: one still image + text caption. No video.**

One photograph per post — the day's machine picture, already in the repo under
the stage hold. Caption carries the Record's headline and one or two of its own
lines, verbatim. Link to `/robots` lives in the bio, not the caption.

Why this and nothing else: the Record entries **are** text and photographs. There
is no video asset for Robots and there is no time to make one. A format that
requires an asset you do not have is a format that fails in week two.

**MUSIC: vertical video, 30–60 seconds, one continuous take, audio playing.**

Posted as a YouTube Short and the same file as an Instagram Reel. Under 60s so
one file serves both surfaces without a re-cut.

Why: audio is the product. A still image cannot carry a song, so this is the one
place video is not optional.

## 2c. TIMING — the number to hold for ninety days

**4 posts a week, total. 52 posts across the ninety days.**

| Brand | Cadence | Days | 90-day total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Robots** | 3/week | **Mon · Wed · Fri** | 39 |
| **Music** | 1/week | **Saturday** | 13 |

**This is the sustainable number, not the optimal one.** Optimal for a
serialized fiction would be daily, matching the Record. Daily is not sustainable
alone for ninety days, and a cadence that breaks in week three does more damage
than a slower one that holds. **Posting is not publishing** — the Record can
publish on a day you do not post.

**The batching schedule is the part that makes it survivable:**

- **Robots** — schedule **3 weeks at a time (9 posts)**, one sitting of roughly
  30 minutes. That is **5 sittings in the whole ninety days**. Instagram lets you
  queue 75 days ahead, so you could in principle do the entire run in two
  sittings; 3 weeks is recommended so the captions can follow the Record as it
  is actually written.
- **Music** — one video a week is the constraint, not the posting. Batch-shoot
  **4 videos in one session, once a month** (3 sessions in 90 days), and schedule
  the month.

**Launch week exception:** one post from each held Weird.Baby name on
**Sun 2026-08-16 or Mon 2026-08-17** — the museum is open, here is the address.
Then the hold goes quiet and stays quiet.

## 2d. MECHANICS

### The two accounts you will actually post from

**INSTAGRAM — two accounts (Robots, Music) + one hold (Weird.Baby)**

- **Creation:** free. Each account needs its own username and its own email or
  phone at signup. You can then add multiple accounts to a single app login and
  switch between them without logging out (commonly capped around 5) — do this,
  or you will be typing passwords for ninety days.
- **Verification:** no ID. Email or phone confirmation only. The blue check is
  **Meta Verified and is PAID** — I did not verify the current 2026 price, so
  treat any figure you see as unconfirmed. **You do not need it.**
- **The account must be PUBLIC or the scheduler does not appear.**
- **Native scheduling: YES — free, in the app.** Create a Post or Reel, toggle
  **"Schedule this post"**, pick date and time. Reported limits: up to **25
  scheduled posts per day** and up to **75 days in advance**; minimum ~20 minutes
  ahead. As of a **March 2026** change this is open to all **public** accounts,
  not just professional ones.
  **Flag:** every source for those numbers is a marketing blog, and one of them
  gave a conflicting window of 29–30 days rather than 75. Meta's own help page
  returned no content when fetched. **Treat 75 days as unconfirmed and verify it
  on the first real schedule attempt — schedule one post 40 days out and see if
  it takes.**
- **Stories cannot be scheduled natively.** Do not plan any Stories cadence.

**YOUTUBE — one channel for Music (+ hold the Robots and Weird.Baby names)**

- **Creation:** needs a Google account. Create each channel as a **Brand Account**
  under your one Google login — that gives you multiple channels with **no second
  password to keep**. This is the single most useful mechanic here.
- **Verification:** phone verification at `youtube.com/verify` is required for
  custom thumbnails, videos over 15 minutes, and live streaming. Basic Shorts
  upload and scheduling reportedly do not need it. **Do it once anyway on day
  one** — you will want custom thumbnails eventually and you do not want to
  discover the block mid-batch.
- **Native scheduling: YES — free, YouTube Studio on desktop.** Upload, choose
  **Schedule** instead of Publish, pick date and time. Regular videos up to
  ~**12 months** ahead. Shorts reportedly up to **6 months**.
  **Flag:** the 6-month Shorts figure comes from a marketing blog, not Google
  documentation. Could not verify.
- **Use desktop, not the mobile app** — mobile scheduling support is limited.

## 2e. MUSEUM-SHAPED vs MUSICIAN-SHAPED

| | **Museum-shaped** | **Musician-shaped** |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | an **object** | a **person** |
| Voice | third person, or no person at all | first person |
| Frame | same crop, same ground, one object per post | face and voice present, varied |
| Caption | a **label** — what it is, when it arrived | an **ask** — listen, pre-save, come to the show |
| Time | **accrues.** Scrolling back finds a catalogue | **spikes.** A release peaks, silence between is normal |
| Cadence | stability matters more than volume; a quiet month is not a fault | event-driven; gaps are expected and forgiven |
| Follow reason | the series | the person |

**What it means for these two, and it is the whole argument for 2a:**

**Robots is museum-shaped.** The Record already *is* a dated catalogue — one
object, one entry, one date, accruing. Its account should be near-boring on
purpose: same frame, label captions, fixed cadence. A viewer who scrolls back
through it finds the volume.

**Music is musician-shaped.** First person, audio-first, and it asks for
something.

**They cannot share one account** because the two shapes disagree on the one
thing an account cannot be ambiguous about: **whether the subject is an object or
a person.** Every other difference follows from that. This is why the separate-
accounts answer in 2a is mechanical rather than a preference.

**And Weird.Baby the house is museum-shaped too — which is exactly why it can
hold and not post.** A museum that says nothing for a month is not broken. A
musician who says nothing for a month has stopped.

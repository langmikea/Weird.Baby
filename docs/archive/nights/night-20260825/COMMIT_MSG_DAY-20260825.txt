A SECTION IS A HEADER AND ITS LINES, AND THAT IS THE TERMINOLOGY RATHER THAN A
DESCRIPTION OF ONE. His words: "SECTIONS exist. They start with a HEADER which
always fits the constraints and displays per the standard recipe. The text
following the header is the LINES, always formatted the same, indented, etc."
Two functions, headerHtml and linesHtml, and no third path anywhere on the page.
The LINES are monospace and pre-wrap on the data's own evidence - Record 004's
folder tree hangs PORTAL.CFG under TERMINAL.EXE at column 26 and 002's addendum

show him a shape the section does not have. And his paragraphs are NOT joined -
each run of lines draws as its own block through the same recipe, because
joining them would be Ops smoothing his writing.

THE VIEWER'S FINAL STEP IS x2 AND IT IS A SECOND IMAGE, NOT A SCALE. "It is not
enough today" was measuring something real: the sources are 1700x2200, VIEW_PX
fits them inside 480, so the viewer drew 371x480 - its own natural size. Scaling
that up would show exactly the detail x1 already showed, at twice the size, and
say it was showing more. So the day's pictures are inlined twice, at 480 and at
960, and the step swaps the source. Measured served: x1 371x480 natural, x2
742x960 natural - naturalWidth actually doubles. The cost was measured before it
was spent, ~45KB of base64 each against ~20KB. The shelf gets no second copy -
138 tiles would be about eight megabytes to answer a question the light table
already answers - so x2 is DISABLED for a shelf picture and its hint says why,
rather than upscaling and calling it a zoom.

THUMBNAILS: FAINT, NOT SMALL, AND THE MEASUREMENT KILLED THE CROP. The manual
pages are 2040x2640, mean luminance 246-250 of 255, with 91-97% of every page in
the brightest band and ink below 200 at 0.3%-1.8% of pixels. The ink's bounding
box is 61-92% of the page, so cropping to it buys x1.00 to x1.24; sharp.trim() is
worse at 98.4-99.7%; and no single band is representative, the densest 25%
holding 34-51%. So: a percentile contrast stretch at a bigger size. On page-07
ink goes 1.8% -> 9.8% at normalise 2/98; 1/99 is weak at 6.8% and 5/95 blows out
at 88.9%. normalise() is NOT a min/max stretch, which is why it works - these
pages already hold a true 0 and a true 255.

IT IS APPLIED PER IMAGE ON THE IMAGE'S OWN EVIDENCE AND NEVER TO A PHOTOGRAPH.
stretch:"auto" reads each image's greyscale mean and stretches only above 235: a
manual page is 246, the machine photograph front_screen.png is 108, so the gate
skips every photograph by a margin of 127. The cache key carries the size and the
treatment, so 480 and 960 of the same file are two entries and neither can serve
the other's request. lighttable.mjs's change is additive - both existing callers
pass no stretch and are byte-unchanged.

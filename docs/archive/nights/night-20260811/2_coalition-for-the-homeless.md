## 2a — WHAT THEY ARE

**Founded 1981. Forty-five years old.** Founded by **Robert Hayes**, a lawyer
who two years earlier had brought the case that changed New York.

They are unusual in being **both an advocacy organisation and a direct-service
one** — they sue the city, and they also drive the vans. Most charities are one
or the other.

**Twelve frontline programmes**, in four groups: emergency services (the food
vans, the CARE programme), crisis services (eviction prevention, an emergency
mail address for people with no address), housing (three programmes), and job
training and youth work (including a summer camp).

**Scale:** they serve **over 3,500 people a day**. Revenue for the year ending
June 2025 was **$21.5m** against **$21.6m** of expenses — they spent what they
took in. Net assets $44.4m. The executive director, David Giffen, was paid
$271,465, which is unremarkable for a $21m New York non-profit.

**The context they work in, from their own June 2026 reading:** 95,994 people
slept in New York City shelters that night. 30,739 of them were children.

## 2c — WHAT A MUSEUM SHOULD KNOW BEFORE POINTING ITS MONEY

### The ratings are clean

**Charity Navigator: 96%, four stars.** Every accountability metric scored 100% —
independent audit, audit oversight committee, no diverted assets, no loans to
officers, records retention policy, tax form published.
([Charity Navigator](https://www.charitynavigator.org/ein/133072967))

**Caveat, stated plainly:** only one of Charity Navigator's four assessment areas
has been completed for them. Impact & Measurement, Leadership & Planning, and
Culture & Compensation are all marked incomplete. **The 96% is a real score for
accountability and finance and is silent on whether the programmes work.**

### The finances are unremarkable in the good way

$21.5m in, $21.6m out for FY2025 — a $27k shortfall on a $21m budget. Five-year
revenue $19–28m, expenses $15–22m, no year in real trouble. The 2022 spike
($27.8m revenue against $16.1m expenses) is pandemic-era support, and they built
reserves rather than spending it, which is the responsible read.
([ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/133072967))

### The one real thing — the 2024 settlement

**In March 2024 the Coalition signed an agreement with the Adams administration
that lets New York City limit shelter stays for newly arrived adult
asylum-seekers to 30 days, and 60 days for younger adults.** It is an emergency
addendum to the 1981 consent decree — the same decree they won.
([THE CITY](https://www.thecity.nyc/2024/03/15/adult-migrants-limited-to-30-day-stay-in-right-to-shelter-settlement/) ·
[Coalition's own press release](https://www.coalitionforthehomeless.org/press/legal-aid-coalition-for-the-homeless-announce-settlement-in-callahan-v-carey-preserving-nycs-right-to-shelter-settlement-protects-the-right-to-shelter-for-anyone-in-need/))

**Their case for it:** the city was pushing to suspend the right to shelter
outright. The settlement kept the 1981 decree standing and blocked any blanket
denial of shelter to a whole class of people. They traded a limit for the
principle.

**The case against it, from other homeless advocates:** it writes into law a
system that treats people differently depending on when they arrived and where
from. Jamie Powlovich of the Coalition for Homeless Youth — a different
organisation — said *"I fear for the worst"* and predicted more people on the
street.
([City Limits](https://citylimits.org/2024/03/19/right-to-shelter-settlement-enforces-unequal-system-critics-say))

**My reading, said plainly:** this is not a scandal and not a governance failure.
It is a hard call made under pressure by the people who had the most to lose from
getting it wrong, and reasonable people who want the same thing disagreed about
it. **It is worth knowing because a reader who follows New York housing may
already know it**, and a museum that names a beneficiary should not be surprised
by its own choice.

### No scandals found

No financial irregularity, no leadership scandal, no regulatory action, no
watchdog downgrade in anything I read. Their tax filings are published, audited
and current.

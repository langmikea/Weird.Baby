# AD-FREE LEGAL EMBEDDABLE PLAYBACK — THE SIZE OF THE KNOB

**R7 of THE PORCH RULINGS, 2026-08-06. A map, not a build.** Mike:
*"after lunch at the soonest, no rush."* Nothing in this document has been
implemented and nothing in it should be until he rules ([M76](OPEN_ACTIONS.md#m76)).

> **THE PRIZE, in his words:** *"if a whole discography can be served cleanly,
> that is a very big knob we can turn at any point to reinvigorate the artist or
> the site."*

**And his own framing is the one to keep hold of: embedding needs no permission
on either platform. This is a SOURCE question, not a rights question.** What
follows is therefore an inventory of what exists, not an argument about what is
allowed.

---

## 0. THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The knob is real, and it is **large for three artists and small for the fourth —
the fourth being the one whose room just went quiet.** Carsie Blanton and Jesse
Welles each have their whole catalogue on their own Bandcamp, free to stream in
an ad-free embed the museum can place today: sixteen releases and seven albums
against the two songs each the wing currently shows. Mikey Mike has no Bandcamp
and no hosted audio anywhere that could be found; his own site's `/music` page
carries no player and no streaming link at all. **Hunter Root's Bandcamp covers
38 of the vault's 93 tracks and adds only 13 the site cannot already reach
through his YouTube channel** — 33 reachable becomes 46, and **47 of his tracks
are reachable through nothing.**

## 1. WHAT A BANDCAMP EMBED ACTUALLY IS — verified, 2026-08-06

| Question | Answer | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Who can create one? | Anyone. The **Share / Embed** control sits under the cover art on every public release page and generates plain iframe HTML; no account, key or artist relationship is involved. | Bandcamp Help, *How do I create a Bandcamp embedded player?*; Bandcamp blog, *Full Tracklist Players* (2010) |
| Where can it go? | *"any website that accepts iframe HTML"* | Bandcamp blog, ibid. |
| Does it carry ads or branding? | No. *"clean, free of any Bandcamp branding, and streamlined for music listening and purchasing"* | Bandcamp blog, ibid. |
| Full tracks or previews? | Full tracklist players exist and play the release's tracks; size, colour, tracklist and merch images are configurable. | Bandcamp Help, ibid. |
| Is there a play limit? | **Yes, and it is the one clause worth reading twice.** The default (and minimum) number of full plays before a fan gets a purchase prompt is **3**. An artist can raise it or **disable it entirely**, from the bottom of their profile page. | Bandcamp Help, *What are streaming limits on Bandcamp?* |
| Do the terms restrict third-party embedding? | Bandcamp's Terms of Use say nothing about it either way — no clause permits it and none forbids it. The mechanism is a first-party product feature. | bandcamp.com/terms_of_use, read 2026-08-06 |
| Exclusive embeds? | A separate feature for pre-orders and press first-listens, domain-restricted by the artist. **Not what this is.** | Bandcamp blog, *exclusive embeds* (2016) |

**The honest hole:** the streaming-limit article does not say whether the 3-play
limit is applied inside a third-party embed or only on bandcamp.com. **Ops did
not test it and will not guess.** If the museum ever builds this, the first thing
to do is play one embedded track four times from a clean browser and watch what
happens on the fourth. It is a ten-minute test and it decides whether an embed is
a jukebox or a sampler.

**The second honest hole:** the limit is the ARTIST's dial, not the museum's. A
knob whose stop is set on somebody else's profile page is a knob the house does
not fully own.

## 3. WHAT ELSE WAS LOOKED AT

- **Artist-hosted audio.** Only `weekendatmikeys.com` was worth checking, since
  the other three sites all delegate listening to Bandcamp. It has none.
- **Official SoundCloud.** Only Mikey Mike had candidates, and they are
  unverifiable (above). SoundCloud's free tier also serves audio ads on some
  content, which fails the *ad-free* half of the brief before the source question
  is even reached.
- **Spotify / Apple embeds.** Not researched in depth and deliberately so: the
  Spotify embed serves 30-second previews to a logged-out visitor and full tracks
  only to a logged-in Premium account. That is an advertisement for a
  subscription standing in a free museum, which fails the credo on `/booth`
  before it fails anything technical.
- **YouTube.** Already in use, and it is **not** ad-free — that is the thing this
  research was asked to find an alternative to. It stays because it is the only
  channel that reaches Hunter Root's videos and Jesse Welles' weekly songs at all.

## 4. WHAT OPS RECOMMENDS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT DECIDE

**One test before any build:** play a Bandcamp embed four times from a clean
browser and see whether the purchase prompt appears inside the iframe. Everything
below depends on the answer.

If it behaves as a jukebox, the knob is worth turning for **Carsie Blanton and
Jesse Welles** and is worth very little for the other two. If it behaves as a
sampler, it is a nicer-looking version of what the wing already does and the
honest move is to leave the doors as doors.

**What this document does not decide, because none of it is Ops':** whether a
museum wall should carry another company's player at all; whether three songs of
somebody's record is a better exhibit than two; and whether the wing's whole
posture — *a pointer, not a home* — survives a page you can listen to an album on.
That last one is the real question under R7 and it is worth asking out loud before
the ten-minute test, not after.

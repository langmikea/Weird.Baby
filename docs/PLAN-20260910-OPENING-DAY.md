# OPENING DAY — 2026-10-30. The plan from 2026-09-10.

Mike, 2026-09-10 (cleaned): "We are simply not yet capable of running at
scale. Opening Day target: October 30, Halloween, and my cat's birthday.
The problem: I keep overdesigning the 60-day story into a 60-day story. No
one will care. It needs to be an infomercial: sell the features, games,
artifacts. Make it clear that if a story is to be told, this is just a
prologue, a place for the people who want more. A landing link from the
daily promotion, so you can get grounded in the day's new feature. Shooting
videos is a lot harder than shooting videos: framing on a fixed tripod, the
scenery, usable audio, hearing what you really sound like and adjusting;
all doable, but in time. Records and the calendar can be largely created
and managed by Ops. The twin needs updating with all software, and the real
unit. We have enough time and hindsight to burn down anything that needs
burning down. Nothing is sacred. My music needs to be ready to commence a
launch, with a schedule. I shot the Everyman unboxing and the Gambler; CEO
and the man on the inside next; chips and half dollars bought."

Rulings taken: the site says "not open yet" until 10-30 (low priority; done
when the site is next touched). The robots wing is an infomercial for the
device; the story is a prologue for those who want more. Every daily
promotion lands on the site's page for that day's feature. Ops writes the
Records and runs the calendar; Mike rules. The twin and the real unit are
brought to the full software. The music launch gets its own schedule.
Nothing built on the 60-day story is sacred.

## Answered 2026-09-10

1. **SED = Same Except Data.** Mike: "This is how I like to approach all
   problems: structured elemental building blocks used to create scalable,
   sustainable, ever-changing content." Everything below is written that
   way: a block is a template; Mike delivers the data; Ops renders.
2. **Teasers: TBD.** Mike: "I would love it, but I keep over-promising and
   not delivering. I am looking for a ground-up revamp of the entire project,
   so it is a possibility either way." The plan carries them as optional
   until week 4; nothing depends on them.
3. **The burn list is Ops' call.** Mike: "Work with me on what I need to
   deliver, then on how to deliver, and deal with the tools and processes
   behind the scenes."

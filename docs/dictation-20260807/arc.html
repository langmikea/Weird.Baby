The twelve weeks

Twelve rows. Ops’ headline on the left, yours on the right, and it saves as you type. This is the whole arc on one screen, which is the only reason it is its own page — you asked to work all twelve at once.

Read the two marks separately, because they answer different questions. The label at the head of the left column — Ops, or your words — says whose sentence this is. The band under the headline says whether there is anything of yours underneath it. Week one is Ops’ sentence with your material under it; week two is your sentence, carried word for word, and it is the only one. Everything from week four on has nothing of yours under it at all.

None of these twelve has a limit, and there are no counters on this page for that reason. A week headline is a heading on these pages and never becomes a field in the museum — it is not a Record entry’s headline, which does have a budget and does count as you type on the worksheet. Where a box has something to fit, it says so; where it says nothing, there is nothing to fit.

The ten days of weeks one and two are on the worksheet; how any of this works is on the reference page.

                                 SECTION VIII

                                 MAINTENANCE


8-1.    GENERAL.

        This section gives the care of the instrument. What there is of it is
        given here in full, and nothing beside it is required.

        Nothing within the instrument is an operator-serviceable item. The
        cell is not replaceable by the operator and is dealt with at paragraph
        7-19. No cover is to be removed and no adjustment is provided. An
        instrument wanting work of that kind is withdrawn from service and
        returned; see Section XI.

        What maintenance does upon this instrument is keep its condition up.
        Condition is a real state of the instrument and not a manner of
        speaking. The instrument keeps it, reports it among the readings of
        paragraph 8-9, and shows it falling and recovering. It falls with use
        and it is restored by the routine of paragraph 8-7 and by nothing
        else.

        Maintenance is not a repair and does not stand in for one. It restores
        nothing that is broken and puts nothing right that was wrong. An
        instrument in poor condition is not a faulty instrument: it answers as
        it always answered, it withholds nothing, and no function of it is
        closed by neglect. What falls away is the ease of its working, and
        that is what the routine restores.

        Conditions the instrument reports as faults are not the matter of this
        section and are given in Section IX.

8-3.    PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE.

        The whole of the preventive maintenance of this instrument is the
        routine of paragraph 8-7. There is no second procedure and nothing is
        scheduled beside it.

        It is run by the operator. It requires no tool and no material, the
        instrument is not opened for it, and it is done where the instrument
        stands.

8-2

        No interval is prescribed in this paragraph. The instrument advises
        attention when it wants it, and that advice is the schedule: the
        routine is run when the instrument advises it, and at such other times
        as the keeper sees fit. Where a fixed schedule is required it is given
        in Table 8-1.

        The routine may be run when it is not wanted and no harm follows. An
        instrument already in good condition is left in good condition.

        Cleaning is a separate matter and is given at paragraph 8-5.

8-7.    THE MAINTENANCE ROUTINE.

        The routine is entered at the Maintenance row of the Preferences door.
        The door is described at paragraph 6-25.

        The instrument gives the routine upon both glasses. The forward
        display carries the heading and the step in hand. The upper display
        carries the card, which states the routine, gives the instruction for
        the step, and shows the instrument's efficiency as it stands.

        The routine is:

            1  Take the Maintenance row with the shutter.
            2  Set the instrument level and hold it still.
            3  Give three clicks of the shutter when they are called for.
            4  The instrument reports the service complete.

8-3

        At the second step the card carries a gauge which fills while the
        instrument is held still. Movement empties it and the step is begun
        again. No figure is given and none is wanted: the instrument is held
        until the gauge is full.

        At the third step the clicks are given at the shutter and are counted
        as they are made. Movement at this step returns the routine to the
        second, and the clicks are given again.

        The card states each step in the instrument's own words. It shows, in
        turn:

            HOLD THE UNIT STILL.
            NOW: THREE CLICKS.
            SERVICE COMPLETE.

        The routine may be left at any point with the dial. Nothing is lost by
        leaving it and nothing is gained: the condition stands where it stood.

        When the routine is completed the condition is raised and the
        instrument holds the new figure. It is read again at paragraph 8-9.

8-9.    CONDITION READINGS.

        The instrument shows SEE MANUAL upon the upper display when its
        condition has fallen to the point at which attention is advised. This
        paragraph is what it means by it.

        Nothing is wrong. SEE MANUAL is an advice and it is not a fault
        reported: the instrument is working, it will go on working, and it
        withholds nothing while the notice stands. What it is saying is that
        it has been in service for some while without attention and that the
        routine of paragraph 8-7 is now wanted. Run the routine. The notice is
        withdrawn when the condition has recovered.

        Condition is the instrument's account of its own state. It is kept as
        one figure, it is carried across a shutting down, and it falls with
        use: with the times the instrument has been started, the questions put
        to it, the hours it has run, and the corrections it has made of
        itself. It is restored by the routine and by nothing else.

        The readings the instrument gives of its condition are listed in Table
        8-2 with what each is taken from and when each is shown. They are
        reports. None of them requires anything of itself, and where the
        instrument wants attention it advises it.

8-4

8-11.   SERVICE NOTICES.

        The instrument sends the operator a notice of its own accord as it
        accumulates running time. A service notice is a message: it is held
        with the rest of the correspondence, it is read at the Messages door,
        and it is dealt with at paragraph 6-23.

        A service notice marks running time and nothing else. It is not a
        fault reported and it is not an advice that the condition has fallen;
        the instrument sends it whether its condition is good or poor. The two
        are commonly met together and they are not the same notice.

        A notice sent is not sent again. The instrument neither announces its
        arrival nor repeats it, in the manner of paragraph 6-23.

        The attention a service notice asks for is the routine of paragraph
        8-7.

8-5

        Running time is carried among the condition readings as well; see
        paragraph 8-9 and Table 8-2.

# The shoot pilot: the script

Ops, 2026-09-18. Desk row `shoot-pilot-script`. For Mike, to shoot when it
suits him. One item through the still frames, then seven short clips. It is
the smallest real shoot that puts something through every stage of the photo
line and the footage line before any album is shot (SED-AUDIT-20260918
section 4; the frames are SEXY-SHOOT-20260918 section 3).

- **The item:** the Everyday, the unit, nameplate on (ruled 09-12).
- **Also on the table:** its case and cable, its box, one magazine ad.
- **Time, honestly:** 20 to 30 minutes to put the rig up the first time;
  about 20 minutes to shoot. 11 stills, 8 clips.
- **When:** any day. The board needs it by 10-18. If it is in by Thursday
  09-24, the Everyman shoot of 09-27 runs on a rig already proven.
- **Nothing here is final material.** If a frame is poor, shoot it anyway;
  a poor frame is what the pilot is for.

## 1. The rig

- White poster board taped to the table and curved up the wall behind. No
  edge or fold visible behind the item.
- Soft light from two sides: a window and a white card facing it, or two
  lamps through a white sheet. No flash. No ceiling light. One kind of light
  only.
- iPhone 15, the 1x lens, on the tripod. Press and hold on the item to lock
  focus and exposure, then drag the sun down until the white is bright and
  the item's bright spots still show detail.
- White gloves whenever a hand is in the frame. Lint brush and blower first.
- No rule, no scale, nothing else on the white.
- The unit switched off for the whole pilot. Every screen dark.

## 3. The clips (phone SIDEWAYS, 4K at 60, HDR Video off, on the tripod)

Settings › Camera › Record Video: 4K at 60, HDR Video off. The DJI pair off.
Sideways because one wide 4K take gives Ops several upright framings to cut
from. Keep the item in the middle third of the picture.

Shoot in this order. At the start of each clip say its code out loud
("E one"). Nothing else you say is used.

| code | the take | how long |
|---|---|---|
| E1a | camera straight down on the top window, the window a full circle about a third of the picture's height. No hands. Nothing moves. | 10 s |
| E1b | camera square on to the front glass. No hands. Nothing moves. | 10 s |
| E2 | the same camera position as E1a. Gloved hands hold the unit steady, the top window in the picture the whole time. | 60 s |
| E3 | the same again. Five shakes. After each shake hold dead still for a count of three. | about 30 s |
| E4 | camera close on the controls so the control and the gloved fingers fill the picture. The knob scrolled and clicked for 30 s; the igniter clicked five times; the switch thrown on and off three times. One clip or three, either is fine. | about 60 s |
| E5 | the unit alone on a turntable or a lazy Susan, no hands in the picture, camera at the unit's height. Two full turns, slowly (about 15 s a turn). **No turntable:** hands in, turn the unit a quarter turn, hands out, hold for a count of three; do that eight times. | about 40 s |
| E6 | the camera exactly where it stood for E5. Take everything off the white. The empty white, same light. | 10 s |
| E7 | camera at 45 degrees looking down at the white. One continuous take: gloved hands open the box, lift the unit out, lay the case, the cable and the unit side by side. No talking. If the box is not to hand, skip E7; nothing else depends on it. | 30 to 60 s |

**The camera straight down (frame 5, frame 10, E1a, E2, E3):** tip the
tripod over the item if it will reach. If it will not, hold the phone in
both hands with your elbows on the table. The pilot tells us whether
hand-held is steady enough, so either way is a useful answer.

## 4. The drop

Everything, stills and clips, into OneDrive › WeirdBaby › photos › pilot
(its own folder, so the desk does not take the pilot for the Everyman
shoot). Do not rename, crop or edit anything. Then tell Code: **"pilot
in"**.

## 5. What Ops does with it, within two days

| what came in | the stage it goes through | the question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| frame 1 | the manifest row is written from the slate card | does the card carry enough |
| frames 2 to 9, 11 | the `white` treatment in `tools/photos-build.py` (owed 09-26): find the item, clean the ground to white, square crop | does this rig's white clean up without hand work |
| frame 10 | the same treatment on a flat sheet | does a white sheet on a white ground still find its edge |
| frame 2 | the Everyday's album cover on the dev server | the cover, as a real picture |
| E1a, E1b | the glass finder (`tools/reels-qa.py`), then one Q&A reel built on the new plate | is the static plate better than the two clips in hand |
| E2, E3 | added to the Q&A hold and shake library | how many steady windows a 60 s take yields |
| E4 | one feature-reel cut with the controls in it | is the framing close enough |
| E5 with E6 | the unit masked off the white using the clean plate | does it mask cleanly; turntable or quarter turns |
| E7 | filed for the shoot reel; its story is written first (`shoot-reel-story`, 09-29) | nothing yet |

One page comes back: per stage, worked or broke, and the one change to the
rig or to this script, if any. Only after that page are the four album
shoots sized and dated (pilot before volume).

## 6. Ops calls made here (say so if any is wrong)

- The pilot item is the unit, with the case for the "open" frame and one ad
  for the flat frame, so every kind of frame is shot once.
- Clips are shot sideways, 4K. The sexy-shoot page said upright; the audit
  of 09-18 found that a wide locked-off take serves the templates better.
  The unboxing is included in that: it is cut upright from the wide take.
- The element clips replace the short finished shots V1 and V3 of the
  sexy-shoot page (the ten-second turn in hands, the three drifting passes):
  drifting hand-held passes are the hardest footage to mask. V2, the
  unboxing, stands as E7.
- The unit is off throughout: every screen the reels show is drawn by the
  twin and set into the dark glass.

The Glass Fit

The machine reveals its answer in FreeSansBold9pt7b on a 128 by 64 glass. In that face a row holds about ten capitals or fourteen small letters, and the firmware draws three rows at most. The answer tables were fitted to a 32-character rule written for the small font, so most of them do not fit the reveal. Below, the Gambler's twenty as the firmware draws them today, beside a re-cut that fits.

Fit as marked: the cell's own line breaks, every row within 128 px. Re-broken: the same words, broken by the machine's font instead of the character count. Sentence case: re-broken with the capitals lowered, which is how the CEO and Informer tables were typed and why they mostly fit. Need a cut: words have to go.

The Gambler's twenty

Left, the cell as it stands (the samples were typed in capitals); the glass shows what the firmware would draw: text past the edge is cut and its tail drops a row. Right, Ops' re-cut in sentence case, three rows or fewer, every row measured in the machine's font. Where a word of his had to go it is named in red. The ruling, Sunday · one letter

  1. A. The glass rules the words. Every answer in every table is re-cut to three rows of the reveal font: this Gambler draft goes in as is or with your changes, and Ops re-breaks the other three tables by the font and drafts the cells that need a cut. The reels show exactly what the unit shows.
  2. B. The words rule the glass. The reveal font changes to the small face the fit rule was written for (about twenty-one characters a row); every cell fits as written, and the reviewed reveal look changes on the unit and in the reels.
  3. C. Two screens. The reels wrap freely, as they do now, up to four lines; the unit keeps the tables as written and clips until the bench trip. The reels and the unit no longer show the same thing.

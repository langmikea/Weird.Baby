The Elements

Reels assembled from building blocks: a library of fixed shots of each unit, plus one line of data a day. Your idea, read against what we own and what exists. Nothing here is built until it is ruled.

Photos and videos of elements: a front view, an isometric view, a back view, the unit in the hand, a finger on the scroll knob and on the button. Remove the friction from making a video once a question and an answer are chosen. A simulated screen might be acceptable, or the VIIIp flashing generic imagery. Planned well, it looks like a multi-camera shoot. Think broadly: the music videos too. Do not reinvent the wheel.

The glassthree ways, and one not to take

A
The twin's display, composited into a real plate.

One static close-up plate of each unit's glass, shot once. The twin renders the answer; the line composites it at the measured aperture with the CRT's own bloom. Honest by construction: the machine's own software showing the machine's own answer. Forcing an answer is a setting, never a shoot. Recommended.

B
The real unit in attract mode, the twin for the close-up.

Flash the unit with a generic moving pattern so the hand and knob elements have a live glass; use A for the answer shot. Needs the firmware flash, on the calendar for 10-15 with you at the bench.

C
The real unit, every day.

The current plan and the friction you are escaping. Keep it for the occasional true one-take.

×
Not the AI-simulated screen.

It would invent what the machine says on the glass, the one thing the house never does. And it is unnecessary: the twin is the real screen.

The multi-camera look

Shoot once per unit, one afternoon: front, isometric, back, in hand, finger on the scroll knob, finger on the igniter, the shake, and the static glass plate. Each as a photograph and as three to five seconds of video with a little life in it. Then three or four cut lists, not one, so no two days are the same cut: wide, insert, knob, glass with the answer, hold, pop. Fifteen seconds. The trade's own rule is the same: a tagged library of a handful of angles per subject, two or three takes each, turns an hour's edit into minutes.

The engine

A
Ours.

ffmpeg, already in the line, plus a headless browser to render the twin's glass. No vendor, no fee, no new format.

B
Remotion.

Video from React, the museum's own stack; the twin's display could sit inside a composition directly. Free for individuals. More work, more motion.

C
A hosted template API.

Creatomate or Shotstack: JSON timelines rendered in the cloud. Clean, paid, a vendor between us and the file; nothing here needs one yet. Phone template apps are hand work every day, which is the friction being removed.

The risk to name

Both platforms now throttle repetitive, templated patterns and reward original footage. So the elements are real footage of the real object and the real hands; several cut lists; one fresh shot a week; clean files, never a cross-posted watermark. The Determination stays a real question with a real answer. Only the pictures around it are assembled.

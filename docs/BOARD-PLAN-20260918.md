## 2. The plan's shape (the wheel, not reinvented)

Four levels. The exec sees the top three; tasks are Ops' and stay below.

```
LAUNCH        /M the Number 10-26 noon · /R the door 10-30 at five   (intent and purpose: ruled drafts, LAUNCH-20260918)
  OUTCOME     a testable sentence that is true on the day, or is not
    COLUMN    a family of deliverables (the board's columns)
      DELIVERABLE   one must-have thing, with an owner, gates, a need-by date, and what it waits on
        task        dated work (TASKS.json); every task names its deliverable, or is challenged
```

Borrowed practice, by name:

- **Work breakdown structure**: the levels above. Deliverables, not activities.
- **Weighted milestones** (earned value's rules of credit): percent complete
  is gates passed, never an estimate. Credit is earned at a gate, nothing
  in between.
- **Baseline and forecast**: need-by dates are planned backwards from the
  launch once, then held. Status is forecast against that baseline, not
  against last week, so a thing cannot slip a little every week and stay
  green.
- **Written, objective status rules**: the cure for the "watermelon" (green
  outside, red inside). The owner does not pick the colour; the rule does.
- **Management by exception**: the board shows what is off; the exec rules
  the exception (3C). Every ruling goes in one decision log.

## 5. Gates: what percent complete means

Each family has one gate ladder (SED: same ladder, different data). A
deliverable earns a gate's weight when the evidence exists: a file in the
tree, a row in a ledger, a ruling on record. Nobody types a percentage.

```
REEL TYPE   story ruled 10 · template ruled 25 · line builds unattended 20 · one real post through Buffer 15 · launch content in hand 30 (counted, e.g. 9 of 15)
PROGRAM / GAME   pitch ruled 10 · final on the twin 40 · page live in development 30 · proven on the real unit 20
ALBUM       shot 30 · photographs through the line 20 · album filled (cover, plates, kit, papers) 35 · ruled 15
SITE PIECE  shape ruled 20 · built 40 · in the launch build 20 · passed the rehearsal 20
MACHINE     plan and date 10 · done 60 · verified against the audit 30
SYSTEM      built 40 · proven once for real 40 · ran unattended a week 20
```

Weights are Ops' draft. They matter less than the rule that credit comes
only at a gate.

## 6. The status rule (binary, written, no amber)

Every gate gets a need-by date, planned backwards from its launch with the
freeze (10-26) as the wall. A deliverable that is not done is **not on
track** if any one of these is true; otherwise it is on track:

1. a gate's need-by date has passed;
2. it is blocked, and the unblocking has no committed date, or a date after
   the need-by;
3. the work left does not fit before the need-by at the rate shown so far
   (fifteen Q&As, four Sundays of shooting);
4. something it depends on is not on track;
5. no open task serves it: nobody is scheduled to do it (added at the
   build, 09-18; it is the rule that ties the calendar to the plan).

Rule 4 is why a shared blocker (the Buffer key, "the games are not final",
the bench) lights every column it touches. That is intended: the same name
in five pop-ups is the signal.

## 7. The board

One column per row of section 4. Each column is 100 percent of its
must-have weight, stacked: **done** (gate credit earned), **on track**
(the rest of the on-track deliverables), **not on track** (the rest of the
others). Click a red block, get lines in the ruled format and nothing else:

```
NAME - blocker (owner) - needed by MM-DD
```

No green detail, no history, no tasks. Expect the first honest board to be
redder than the desk's "nothing late"; that is the point of rules 2 to 4.

## 8. What changes in how we run

- **One plan file** (data) holds outcomes, columns, deliverables, gates,
  need-by dates, owners and depends-on. The board, the desk and the morning
  run read it. PLAN-20260910 is marked superseded, not rewritten.
- **The catalogue gains status** through the program/game ladder.
- **Every task names its deliverable.** A task that serves none is
  challenged, not scheduled.
- **The Sunday grade becomes the exception sitting**: gates passed this
  week, then each red ruled under 3C (cut it, move the date, add effort, or
  accept it), written to a decision log.
- **Mike's deliverables are on the board like anyone's.** The owner column
  is how the exec sees where he himself is the constraint.

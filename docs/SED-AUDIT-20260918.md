## 2. Why Mike's lane reads 0 of 14, and what it actually says

The launch plan's R1 called this a delivery risk. That is the wrong reading
and it is withdrawn. The record: the practice reels, the element takes, the
teaser and launch shoots, the weekly questions were all asked for in volume,
and each was overtaken when a small bite showed a better way (the Q&A line
needs no daily shoot; the porch tape already held the Number; the tray
became the sexy shoot). The asks were not failed; they were voided by
learning. **The fault is the plan's: it asked for volume before any line had
run once, end to end, on a real input.** Every one of his fourteen open rows
today is still a volume ask on an unpiloted line.

The rule that follows: **no volume ask of Mike until one real unit has gone
through that line's whole pipe** (input, build, page or post), and the pilot
is the smallest input that exercises every stage.

## 4. Is the necessary shoot scripted?

For stills: yes. SEXY-SHOOT-20260918 is a proper SED grammar (S0 to S8, the
same frames for every item, the slate writes the manifest).

For footage: **no, and it walked away from Mike's own method.** His shooting
plan of 09-12 (ELEMENTS section 9) is elemental: sixty-second takes (the
knob, the turn, handheld in front of the device), "a virtually bottomless
supply of footage", the static glass plate shot separately for compositing,
repeated in other settings. This morning's shoot plan asks instead for
short, finished-shot video (a ten-second turn, three five-second drifting
passes, one unboxing) for a reel whose story is not written. Drifting
handheld passes are the hardest footage to mask or composite; the glass
finder already showed that (ELEMENTS section 16).

What the automated lines actually consume, and so what an element take list
should be, derived from the templates rather than from a reel idea:

```
E1  the static glass plate   locked off, front glass and top window, screen dark, 10 s      the twin's screen is composited here (Q&A, ask, feature)
E2  the hold                 gloved hands, unit steady, glass in frame, 60 s                  the Q&A library; today 2 clips, one unit
E3  the shake                gloved hands, five shakes with a settle between                  the NIAC reveal beat
E4  the controls             60 s on the knob, scrolled and clicked; the igniter; the switch  "unseen forces" in the feature reels; manual pages
E5  the turn                 on a turntable, no hands, two full turns, 4K landscape           covers, album motion, masks cleanly against white
E6  the clean plate          the empty white set, 10 s, same light                            the mask and the ground clean-up for everything above
E7  the unboxing             one continuous take (already V2)                                 the shoot reel
```

4K landscape on the tripod where the take allows, so one take yields several
9:16 framings (the 09-12 note). E1 to E6 on one unit is about fifteen
minutes with the rig up.

## 5. The proposed re-plan (for ruling; nothing applied)

1. **Pilot first, on every line.** Add one gate to every ladder before
   "launch content in hand": *one real unit through the whole pipe*. Mike's
   asks become: one Q&A (not fifteen); one item through the shoot grammar
   plus E1 to E6 (not forty-five stills); the six Buffer clicks now, because
   every pilot ends at the queue.
2. **Volume only after the pilot passes**, and sized by what the pilot
   taught. The fifteen Q&As, the four album shoots and the story pieces keep
   their place in the plan but lose their dates until their pilot is in.
3. **A computed critical path.** Give every gate a duration and its real
   predecessors, run the forward and backward pass, and let the dates fall
   out with their float. The board then shows float consumed, which is an
   earlier signal than a date passed. The grader already holds the network;
   this is durations and one pass, not a new tool.
4. **The shoot re-scripted around elements**: the stills grammar stands;
   the video section becomes E1 to E7; a fifteen-minute pilot sitting before
   any album's full session.
5. **Riskiest unknowns first.** In order: the queue (one real post); the
   white treatment on a real frame; the real unit's firmware (a bench pilot
   earlier than 10-15 if Mike can, even ten minutes: does it flash); one
   feature page from the catalogue template.

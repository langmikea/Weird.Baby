                                  SECTION VI

                            OPERATING INSTRUCTIONS


6-1.    GENERAL.

        This section describes the instrument in use: the menu system by which
        its functions are reached, the taking of a determination, and the
        settings the operator may work. How the instrument is built is not
        described here and is the matter of Section VII.

        Starting the instrument is the matter of Section V and is not
        repeated. The Bias Settings, and the procedure by which they are
        enabled, set and adjusted, are the matter of Appendix B.

6-3.    THE MENU SYSTEM.

        The functions of the instrument are reached through the menu system.
        It opens at four doors, each named for what stands behind it:
        MGK-VIIIp, Messages, Programs and Preferences. Table 6-1 gives them
        and says where each is described.

        A door opens on a level. A level is a table of rows, and the rows are
        presented one at a time. The dial moves through the rows and does not
        choose; the shutter takes the row the dial has arrived at. A row
        either opens a further level or runs the function it names.

        The MGK-VIIIp door stands before the instrument's own work and has no
        paragraph apart from this one. Behind it are the answer engines, from
        which a determination is taken; the predictions and the probabilities,
        which are drawn rather than determined; the advisory functions, which
        counsel by subject; and the detectors, which report upon what they
        hear. Each is described in its own paragraph below.

    6-4.    Rows, passages, and rows not shown.  A row that returns the

            Not every row of a level is shown. A row appears when the

6-5.    OBTAINING A DETERMINATION.

        The operator puts the question to the instrument and calls for a
        determination.

        The instrument reports that its output is redirected. The
        determination forms at the fluidic suspension and is presented there.
        Figure 6-2 gives the sequence in outline.

6-3

        The determination is given as it stands. The instrument does not
        qualify it and does not account for it, the grounds of a determination
        being no part of what is asked for.

           Figure 6-2.  OBTAINING A DETERMINATION, SIMPLIFIED FLOW

    6-6.    The minimum reading interval.  A determination is held for not
            less than two and a half seconds before a further request is
            accepted. The interval is fixed and is not an adjustment.

            When it has passed the determination stands. It is not withdrawn
            after any period, and the instrument waits.

    6-7.    Requesting a further determination.  A further determination is
            called for by shaking the instrument again.

            A shake made while a determination is still standing does not
            produce another. What is drawn is the determination already given,
            shown a second time. The instrument does not determine a question
            twice, and the determination it has given is the determination
            that stands.

            Shakes made early and repeatedly, before a question has been put,
            are not read as requests. They call instead for the recalibration
            of paragraph 8-7.

6-9.    THE ANSWER ENGINES.

        An engine holds the response set from which a determination is drawn
        and the bias by which it is drawn. The prediction engine receives the
        request and passes it to the engine in use, and the instrument answers
        at that engine until another is taken.

        Engines are fitted as standard. There are further engine positions
        that are not fitted in instruments of general issue, and a position
        not fitted is dealt with at paragraph 6-29.

        The engines, and what distinguishes one from another in presentation,
        are scheduled at Appendix C.

6-11.   PREDICTIONS.

        The predictions are determinations upon what has not yet come to pass.
        They are called for and presented in the manner of paragraph 6-5, and
        the Bias Settings bear upon them as upon any other determination.

        A prediction names no event and fixes no time. What the instrument
        offers under this head is its reading of what stands before the
        operator, and it is to be read as a determination and not as a report.

6-13.   PROBABILITIES.

        Under this head the instrument stands in the place of an object: a
        coin, a card, a pair of dice. An operator with no such object to hand
        has the instrument instead, and the instrument is put to the same use.

        What is given is a plain mechanical selection. It is not drawn from a
        response set, nothing in Appendix B bears upon it, and it is not a
        determination. The instrument neither leans toward the selection it
        makes nor accounts for it.

6-15.   ADVICE.

        The advisory functions counsel by subject. What is asked for under
        this head is not a determination: a determination settles a question
        put to the instrument, and counsel bears upon a situation the operator
        is already in.

        The distinction governs how the two are read. A determination is given
        and stands. Counsel is offered upon a state of affairs the instrument
        has not been told the whole of, and the operator, who has, is left to
        weigh it.

6-5

        An advisory function not fitted to the instrument is dealt with at
        paragraph 6-29.

6-17.   DETECTORS.

        A detector reads the room and not the question. The reading is taken
        from the sound about the instrument, through the sound input, and it
        is taken while the subject speaks.

        The needle rises with what is heard and the verdict is reported when
        the reading is complete. The deflection is a true measure of the
        sound; the verdict is a measure of nothing, and the detectors are a
        novelty and to be treated as such.

6-19.   DIVERSIONS.

        The diversions are provided for the idle instrument. They occupy the
        operator and they determine nothing: an instrument that diverts is not
        answering, and no diversion draws upon a response set or upon the Bias
        Settings.

        A score is kept for the session in which it is made and is not carried
        to the next.

6-21.   ANCILLARY PROGRAMS.

        The ancillary programs are the code utilities. They are provided for
        service use and take no part in the determination.

        The built-in self test, and each of the utilities in turn, are the
        matter of Section X and are not described here. The codes by which
        they are reached, and the manner of entering and retaining a code, are
        the matter of Appendix A.

6-23.   MESSAGES.

        The instrument maintains correspondence of its own. What stands under
        this head are messages the instrument holds for the operator, and not
        messages the operator composes; no provision is made for composing
        one.

        Certain items are present from installation. They were not sent and
        have always been there. Others arrive when the instrument has cause to
        deliver them: an item that has not arrived is not listed, and the
        instrument neither announces an arrival nor repeats it.

        Table 6-2 gives the classes under which correspondence is kept, the
        states a message stands in, and how each is made known.

6-25.   PREFERENCES.

        The Preferences door carries what the operator may set. The Bias
        Settings are set from it and are the matter of Appendix B, where the
        manner of enabling, setting and adjusting them is given; it is not
        repeated here.

        Also set from this door are the output mode, the enunciator voice, and
        the operator record. The operator record is the matter of paragraph
        6-27.

6-27.   THE OPERATOR RECORD.

        The instrument keeps a record of the operator: a name to address the
        operator by, and the date from which a reading proper to the operator
        is taken. Both are entered at this door and both stand until they are
        changed.

        Where no name is on file the instrument does not invent one and does
        not proceed as though it had one. Every surface that would carry a
        name states instead that none is held, and the instrument addresses
        the operator as it must until a name is entered.

        The record is a record and it is not an acquaintance. Nothing kept
        here is turned to any other end: a name is held so that the operator
        may be addressed by it, and a determination is what the question and
        the Bias Settings produce and nothing else. What the instrument has of
        the operator it has been given.

6-29.   FUNCTIONS NOT FITTED.

        A row may be present and lead nowhere. Where the menu system names a
        function that is not fitted to the instrument, the row stands and is
        shown, and the instrument states plainly upon reaching it that the
        function is not installed.

6-7

        An engine position not fitted is met in the same manner and is
        scheduled at Appendix C.

        A row that is not shown at all is a different matter and is dealt with
        at paragraph 6-4. A function not fitted is absent from the instrument
        and says so; a row not shown is present and waits upon cause.
